Mould on Walls in Bali: Remove It and Keep It Away

Mould on your Sanur villa walls is rarely just a cleaning problem – it is usually an airflow or hidden-moisture issue wearing a cosmetic disguise. The sea breeze, months of steady rain, and older construction with thin plaster over brick create ideal conditions. Strip the growth properly, fix what is feeding it, and choose finishes that can actually handle this climate.

Why Mould Keeps Coming Back

In Sanur the air carries salt moisture all year round, and from November through March the rains are long and heavy. Older villas were often built with a single coat of cement plaster directly on brick, no vapour barrier, and minimal thought given to drainage behind external walls. That combination means any small leak – a cracked grout line, an overflowing rooftop tank, a slow PDAM pipe joint – wicks water into the wall and feeds fungal growth within days.

Indoors, enclosed bathrooms and kitchens without a working exhaust fan trap steam against cool plaster. Add an AC unit running most of the day and you get condensation dripping onto the ceiling corner. None of these sources is dramatic; they are small, persistent, and easy to miss until the black spots appear.

  • Check that bathroom and kitchen exhaust fans actually pull air outside rather than recirculating it
  • Look for damp patches near the base of external walls after heavy rain – that points to a drainage or pointing issue
  • Inspect your rooftop tank lid and overflow pipe; a blocked overflow seeps along the roof edge and stains the ceiling below

How to Strip Mould Off Walls Properly

Wear a dust mask and rubber gloves, open every window in the room, and lay a towel on the floor. For painted or plastered surfaces, scrub the affected area with a cloth soaked in undiluted white vinegar or a 3-percent hydrogen-peroxide solution. Let it sit ten minutes, wipe clean with fresh water, and let the wall dry completely before doing anything else.

Do not mix chlorine bleach with vinegar, ammonia, or any other cleaner. The fumes are genuinely dangerous in an enclosed room. If you smell something sharp while cleaning near water outlets, stop, ventilate, and call a professional rather than pushing through.

If the mould has eaten into the plaster and the surface feels soft or crumbly, scraping alone will not fix it. That layer needs to be cut back to solid material, treated, and re-plastered. Fixing the Airflow Problem

The single most effective change is getting stale, humid air out of the building. In a typical Sanur villa that means two things: a properly sized exhaust fan in the bathroom (and ideally the kitchen) that vents outside, and a habit of opening opposite windows so air actually crosses the room instead of swirling in place.

During the wet season, when outdoor humidity stays high for weeks, cross-ventilation helps less. A small dehumidifier in the main living area takes the edge off condensation without changing your routine. Also run the AC condensate drain line once a month; a blocked drip tray quietly floods the wall behind the unit.

  • Run the bathroom exhaust fan for at least ten minutes after every shower, even if you open the window
  • Keep gutters and downpipes clear before November so roof water does not run behind external walls
  • If your villa has an enclosed courtyard or carport, make sure there is at least one high-level vent to let hot, moist air escape

Materials That Hold Up in This Humidity

When re-plastering or tiling a damp-prone area, skip standard gypsum board and go with cement-fibre board – it will not swell or delaminate if splashed. For paint, choose a product labelled anti-mould rather than a generic emulsion; the difference is a biocide additive that stops spores from colonising the film.

Around tiles, use silicone-based sealant instead of acrylic. Acrylic dries hard and cracks within a year or two in this climate; silicone stays flexible and keeps water out of the joint. Re-seal grout lines annually with a penetrating sealer – it is a twenty-minute job that saves a full re-grout later.

Keeping Mould From Returning

Prevention here is mostly about catching small moisture sources before they become wall problems. Wipe down bathroom tile grout once a week during the wet season. Check your rooftop tank lid, overflow pipe, and flashings around any roof penetrations monthly. If you notice a musty smell in one room after rain, trace the damp patch back to its source while the wall is still wet – it is far easier to fix then.

For older villas on PDAM supply, consider fitting a pressure-reducing valve near the meter. Sudden pressure spikes can crack internal joints over time, and those slow leaks are a classic hidden mould feeder behind what looks like a perfectly dry wall.

  • Once a month: run all taps for thirty seconds to flush mineral build-up and check under sinks for weeping
  • Before rainy season: clear gutters, inspect external pointing, and make sure the septic-tank area drains away from the foundation
  • If black spots reappear within three weeks of cleaning, stop repainting and look for the water source first

Questions

Can I just paint over the mould to hide it?

No. Paint seals spores into the surface without killing them. In this humidity they push through the new coat within a few weeks, often worse than before. Always strip, treat, let dry fully, then repaint with an anti-mould product.

Is chlorine bleach safe for mouldy walls in Bali?

It can kill surface growth but does not penetrate plaster well, and the fumes are harsh in a small enclosed room. More importantly, never mix it with vinegar or ammonia – the gas produced is dangerous. If you prefer bleach, use it undiluted on non-painted surfaces only, ventilate hard, and rinse thoroughly.

How do I tell if the moisture is coming from inside the wall or outside?

Look at the pattern. Damp that appears after rain along the base of an external wall usually points to ground drainage or pointing failure. Damp near a ceiling corner after a shower or beside a tap suggests an internal pipe or tank leak. If unsure, send me a photo on WhatsApp and I'll walk you through where to look first.
