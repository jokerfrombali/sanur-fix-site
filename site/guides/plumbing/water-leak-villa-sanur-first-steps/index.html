Water Leak in Your Villa? What to Do Before the Plumber Arrives

A burst pipe under the bathroom floor at two in the morning is not a drill — in a Sanur villa with concrete slabs and ageing plumbing, water spreads fast and quietly. Here is exactly what to do in the first ten minutes so you limit damage and make the technician's job quicker. No panic, no guessing — just a short checklist you can follow while your hands are still shaking.

First Ten Minutes: Shut Off Water and Electricity

Your very first move is to stop the flow. Find the main isolation valve (where it hides is covered below) and turn it clockwise until it stops. If the leak is isolated to one fixture — the kitchen sink or the shower head — closing that local tap may be enough, but when in doubt, kill the whole supply.

Next, if water is pooling near any socket, switchboard, or appliance, go to your electrical panel and flip the breaker for that circuit. Do NOT reach into standing water to unplug anything. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave the area immediately and call a professional.

While you wait, lay towels or old sheets over the wet spot to slow the spread across tile or hardwood. If a rooftop tank feeds the house, open the drain cock at the bottom of the tank to relieve pressure and cut the volume reaching the pipes.

  • Close the main valve or the nearest local tap.
  • Switch off the relevant circuit breaker if water touches electrics.
  • Lay absorbent material over the leak point.
  • Open the rooftop-tank drain cock to reduce supply volume.

Where Is the Main Valve in Your Villa?

In most Sanur villas built before the 2010s, the PDAM inlet pipe comes up through the ground floor near the kitchen or back corridor. The isolation valve is usually a brass or blue-handled wheel on a short stub of pipe, sometimes tucked behind a small access panel or under the sink cabinet.

If your villa runs on a bore well instead of PDAM — common in older compounds along Jalan Sanur Kauh or the beachfront strip — the pump controller box often has a ball-valve right beside it. Turn that off first; it stops the well from pushing more water into the system.

Rooftop tanks are everywhere here because PDAM pressure drops during the dry season. The tank's outlet valve sits at the bottom of the tank, accessible from the roof or a ladder hatch. Closing it isolates the house even if the main valve is seized.

Once you find the valve, take a photo and stick a note on the wall. In Bali's heat and humidity, valves can seize after a few months unused, so test them gently every time you prep for the rainy season.

How to Find the Source of the Leak

With the supply cut, walk the affected area with a torch (your phone light works fine). Look for damp patches, white mineral deposits, or a musty smell that points to where water has been travelling.

Check the usual suspects first: the joint where the shower arm meets the wall, the flexi-hose under the basin, the overflow pipe behind the toilet cistern, and the P-trap under the kitchen sink. In older villas, rubber washers inside those connections degrade faster because of the salt air drifting in off the beach.

If the ceiling below is wet but the floor above looks fine, the leak is in the slab or a concealed pipe run. Do not start prising up tiles yourself — you risk cracking the concrete and making the repair more expensive. Mark the spot with tape and move on.

Listen. Even with every tap closed, a slow drip from a cracked pipe will make a faint ticking if you press your ear near the wall. A stethoscope app on your phone can help you pinpoint it.

  • Trace dampness upward — leaks travel down, so the source is usually higher than the stain.
  • Inspect flexible hoses, P-traps, and cistern fill-valves before anything else.
  • Do not pry up tiles or break into walls; mark the area and let the technician open it properly.
  • Use a torch and listen for dripping in a quiet room.

When You Need an Emergency Call

Call immediately rather than waiting until morning if water is still flowing after you have closed every valve you can find, if the leak sits under a load-bearing slab, or if electrical equipment has been submerged. In those cases continued pressure can shift foundations or create a shock hazard that no amount of towels fixes.

If the burst is in a shared wall between two units in a compound villa, notify your neighbour and the property manager right away so both sides can document damage before repairs begin.

During the rainy season (roughly November to March) even a small pinhole leak can flood a ground-floor room within hours because external drainage is already saturated. Treat any active drip as urgent rather than something to deal with on Friday.

  • Water still running despite all valves closed — call now.
  • Electrics submerged or sparking — leave the area, call a professional.
  • Leak in a shared or structural wall — inform neighbours and manager same day.
  • Rainy-season flooding risk — do not wait; call today.

What to Photograph for the Technician

Before you mop up, take four or five clear photos: the wet patch itself, the nearest pipe joint or fitting, the main valve position (open or closed), and any visible corrosion or mineral build-up. A short ten-second video panning across the area helps even more.

Include one wide shot showing where the leak sits relative to the room — sink, shower, toilet — so the technician can gauge access difficulty without guessing over the phone.

Send those images along with your villa address and which floor is affected. The technician can look at the photos, identify likely parts, and quote from what they see before driving out. You avoid paying for a visit that turns into a twenty-minute 'it looks fine' checkup.

Questions

Can I just open the window and let it dry instead of calling someone?

If the supply is fully shut off and only a small amount of water escaped, air-drying plus dehumidifying is reasonable. But if you cannot confirm the source is sealed, or if water reached electrical wiring, get a professional in. In Bali's humidity, moisture trapped under concrete slabs breeds mould within days.

My PDAM bill jumped after the leak — will the plumber help me sort it out?

The technician can document the repair and provide a written summary of what was fixed and when. That record is useful if you need to file an adjustment request with PDAM. Ask the technician for a simple note covering the job date and scope; any fees or account-specific steps depend on your arrangement with PDAM.

Is it safe to use the pool while there is a plumbing leak upstairs?

Generally yes, as long as the leak is not feeding into the pool's return line and no chlorine products have been mixed with other chemicals during cleanup. If you are unsure whether the leak has contaminated the circulation loop, stop using the pool and call before adding more chemicals.
