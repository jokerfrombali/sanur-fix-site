Replacing a Broken Tile in Your Sanur Villa

A single cracked or fallen tile on your bathroom floor or pool deck doesn't look like a big deal until you nick a toe on the sharp edge or water seeps underneath and starts lifting its neighbours. In Sanur's humid, salt-laden air, old adhesive bonds weaken faster than in drier climates, so one loose tile can quietly become five within a season. The good news: replacing one or two tiles is a straightforward job that a local technician can handle in an afternoon without tearing up the whole floor.

Why Tiles Crack and Fall Off Here

Most of the tile failures I see around Sanur come down to three things: age, moisture, and heat cycling. Older villas built in the 1990s and early 2000s were often finished with thin-set adhesives that simply weren't formulated for constant humidity. Add the sea breeze drifting in through open windows and the daily swing between a 30 °C afternoon and a cold air-conditioned night, and the bond slowly loses its grip.

Pool decks add another layer. Chlorine residue and constant wet-dry cycles eat into grout lines first, then work their way under the tile face. During the rainy season (roughly November to March), standing water on terraces and in bathroom corners accelerates the process even further.

  • Salt-laden coastal air degrades cement-based adhesive over time
  • Repeated wet-dry cycles around pools and showers weaken the substrate
  • Older villas may have been laid with outdated or insufficient adhesive
  • Minor structural settling in older buildings shifts tiles off their bed

Choosing the Right Replacement Tile

The single most important step before any repair is matching the existing tile. Size, thickness, surface finish (matte, polished, textured) and colour all need to line up, especially if the tile sits in a high-traffic area like an entryway or pool surround.

If you still have a spare from the original installation, great – bring it along. If not, take a photo of the affected area plus one intact neighbouring tile in natural light. A technician can often source a close match from local stockists in Denpasar or from leftover batches at villa-supply shops along Jl. Raya Sanur. For pool decks specifically, insist on an anti-slip rated tile; smooth porcelain looks nice but becomes dangerously slick when wet.

Replacing the Tile: Step by Step

First, carefully remove any remaining broken shards. Wear gloves and safety glasses – old ceramic edges are razor-sharp. Pry out the loose tile with a flat chisel and a small hammer, working from the edge inward so you don't crack the tiles next door.

Scrape away old adhesive until the substrate (concrete screed or backer board) is clean and sound. If the base is crumbling or hollow, that section needs to be re-levelled before anything new goes down. Apply a fresh layer of polymer-modified thin-set adhesive with a notched trowel, press the new tile firmly into place, and check it sits flush with its neighbours using a straightedge or a level.

If the tile is in a bathroom or shower area and there are electrical outlets or light fixtures within reach of the work zone, switch off the circuit breaker first. Water plus live electrics is not a risk worth taking while you're scraping and mopping up slurry.

  • Remove debris and old adhesive completely; do not lay over a soft or hollow base
  • Use a notched trowel for even adhesive coverage
  • Set the tile at the same height as adjacent tiles to avoid a trip hazard
  • Allow the adhesive to cure fully (usually 24 hours) before applying any load

Grouting and Sealing

Once the adhesive has set, mix grout to a smooth paste and press it into the joints with a rubber float, working diagonally across the lines so you don't pull material out again. Wipe away excess with a damp sponge before it skins over, then let it dry for another day.

In wet areas – bathrooms, pool decks, kitchen splashbacks – a penetrating sealant applied over cured grout adds a real layer of protection against Sanur's humidity and chlorine splashes. Try to schedule grouting on a dry stretch rather than the morning after a heavy downpour; if water gets into fresh grout too early, it can wash out the binder and leave weak, powdery joints.

How Long the Job Takes

A single-tile replacement in good condition typically takes two to four hours of hands-on work plus overnight curing. If you need three or more tiles done, or the substrate needs patching, budget a full day. Multiple rooms or an entire pool-deck section will take longer and depend on how much area is affected.

The exact price depends on the size of the job, the type of tile, and whether the base needs repair. The simplest way to get a quote: snap a clear photo of the damaged spot (and one intact neighbouring tile), send it on WhatsApp, and the technician will come back with a number before anyone picks up a hammer.

Questions

Can I just glue one loose tile back instead of replacing it?

If the tile face is intact and only the adhesive has failed, yes – a technician can lift it, clean both surfaces, re-bed it with fresh thin-set, and reglout the edges. But if the tile is cracked, chipped, or the underlying screed is soft, gluing it back will only buy you a few weeks. In that case a full replacement is the honest fix.

What if I can't find an exact matching tile anymore?

It happens more often than you'd think with older Sanur villas where the original batch was made years ago. Options include sourcing from local stockists who may still hold leftover boxes, choosing a very close match in size and finish so the difference reads as intentional variation, or replacing a small cluster of tiles around the gap so the new piece blends into a slightly larger patch rather than standing out on its own.

Is it safe to use the bathroom or pool while the repair is curing?

No. Keep the area dry and foot-traffic-free for at least 24 hours after the adhesive sets, and avoid getting water on fresh grout for another day. For a pool deck, keep swimmers off until the sealant has fully cured – usually 48 to 72 hours. Stepping on uncured adhesive can shift the tile before it bonds properly, and you'll be back to square one.
