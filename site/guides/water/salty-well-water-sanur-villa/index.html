If your tap water in a Sanur villa has started tasting faintly salty or leaving white scale on the kettle, you are almost certainly dealing with seawater intrusion into a shallow borehole. It is not a plumbing fault; it is a hydrogeological one that gets worse every dry season. Below is what is actually happening under your garden, your options for fixing it, and the questions to ask before you sign another lease.

Why Your Water Tastes Brackish

Sanur sits on a narrow strip of land between the beach and the rice fields. Many older villas were built in the 1980s and 1990s when wells were drilled only ten to twenty metres deep. In the dry months (roughly May through September) the freshwater lens drops, and the saltwater wedge from the Indian Ocean pushes further inland. The result: your well pulls water that is technically still drinkable but noticeably brackish, with a metallic aftertaste and poor soap lather.

You will notice it first in small ways. Kettle scale builds up faster. Shower heads clog within weeks. Plants in the garden start yellowing at the leaf tips even though you are watering them daily. If you have a rooftop tank fed by the well, the whole house runs on that slightly salty supply.

  • Taste test: run cold tap for two minutes; if it tastes like a faint ocean breeze, salinity has crept in.
  • Scale check: wipe the inside of your kettle or shower head. White crust that returns every few days is a sign of elevated minerals and dissolved salts.
  • Well depth matters: ask the previous owner or villa manager how deep the borehole goes. Under fifteen metres near the coast is where intrusion risk spikes.

Switching to PDAM Instead of a Well

PDAM (Perusahaan Daerah Air Minum) is the municipal piped-water system that covers most of Sanur's residential streets. The water comes from treated reservoirs upstream and does not carry the salt problem. For many expat households this is the simplest fix: cap off the well line and connect the main feed to PDAM.

The catch is pressure. PDAM mains can be inconsistent, especially during peak afternoon use or when a neighbour is filling their own tank. That is why almost every Sanur villa still keeps a rooftop storage tank, usually 1,000 to 3,000 litres, fed by a small booster pump. You fill the tank overnight when pressure is steadier and draw from it during the day.

If you are considering the switch, send photos of your current plumbing layout and the PDAM meter box on WhatsApp. A technician can tell you whether a simple reconnection will work or whether you need a new inlet valve, a pressure-reducing regulator, or a larger tank. Pricing depends entirely on what is already in place.

Salt Removal Filters

If you prefer to keep using the well (PDAM supply can be intermittent in some lanes), a reverse-osmosis unit under the kitchen sink handles drinking and cooking water. It strips dissolved salts down to near-zero TDS. For showering and laundry, a whole-house softener with an ion-exchange resin helps reduce scale, though it does not remove salt itself; it swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium, which is gentler on fixtures.

Bali's humidity accelerates resin fouling and membrane degradation. Plan to replace RO membranes every twelve to eighteen months and backwash the softener weekly. Keep spare filter cartridges in a dry cupboard; the sea air will degrade them faster if they sit open.

One safety note: never mix chlorine tablets or liquid bleach with any other cleaning product inside your filter housing or tank. The fumes are dangerous in an enclosed utility room. If you smell strong chemical vapour while servicing filters, stop, ventilate, and call a professional before continuing.

  • RO unit: best for tap water you drink or use for coffee. Expect a small waste-water line to drain.
  • Whole-house softener: protects pipes, geyser elements, and washing-machine seals from limescale.
  • Maintenance cadence: check cartridge pressure gauges monthly during the dry season when salinity peaks.

High Groundwater and Your Septic Tank

The same high water table that pushes salt into wells also sits just below most Sanur gardens. In the rainy season (October through April) the ground can stay saturated for weeks. A septic tank that was fine in 2019 may now be partially buoyant, its inlet pipe floating above the sludge layer, or its leach field sitting in standing groundwater instead of percolating into soil.

Symptoms to watch for: sewage odour drifting up through floor drains after heavy rain, slow-draining toilets even though there is no blockage, or a visible mound of wet grass over the soak-away area. Old villas with single-chamber concrete tanks are especially vulnerable because they were not designed for a water table this close to the surface.

If you suspect your septic system is compromised, do not keep flushing large volumes of water hoping it will 'settle.' Excess liquid raises the level inside the tank and can force raw effluent back into the house. Call a licensed technician to inspect the chamber, check the baffle, and assess whether a second chamber or a raised leach bed is needed.

What to Check Before Signing a Lease

Before you hand over a deposit on a Sanur villa, spend one afternoon doing a basic water audit. It takes twenty minutes and can save you months of headaches.

Run every tap for two full minutes and taste the cold water. Open the geyser and let hot water run; salty scale in the heater element will show as white flakes in the drain. Ask the manager for the well depth and when it was last pumped out. Confirm whether PDAM is connected and where the meter box sits. Finally, walk the garden after a rainstorm if possible: look for soggy patches above the septic area and check that no floor drain smells of sewage.

  • Taste-test all taps (cold and hot) before signing.
  • Ask for the well depth in metres and the year it was drilled.
  • Confirm PDAM connection status and tank capacity.
  • Inspect the septic area for standing water or odour after rain.
  • Photograph the plumbing manifold, filter housings, and any visible corrosion so you have a baseline record.

Questions

Is brackish well water safe to drink?

Faintly salty water is usually within WHO guidelines for sodium intake, but if the taste is strong or your TDS reading exceeds 1,500 mg/L, use a reverse-osmosis unit for drinking and cooking. If you notice skin irritation or a metallic film on dishes, stop using that supply for food preparation until a technician tests the line.

Can I just ignore the salt and keep using the well?

Short-term, yes. Long-term, dissolved salts accelerate corrosion of copper pipes, degrade gasket seals, and shorten the life of your geyser element. In Sanur's humid air the problem compounds faster than in drier climates. A softener plus periodic flushing will buy time, but it does not remove the salt itself.

What should I do if I smell sewage near my villa after heavy rain?

Stop using toilets and sinks connected to that line. Do not pour chemicals into drains hoping to mask the smell. Ventilate the area and call a septic-tank specialist to inspect the chamber level and leach field before the next storm. Raw effluent backflow is a health risk, especially for children playing in the garden.
