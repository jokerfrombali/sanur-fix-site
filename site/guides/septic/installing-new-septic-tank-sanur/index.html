Installing a New Septic Tank in Your Sanur Villa

If your villa's old pit is overflowing, smells are creeping into the garden, or renovations are adding bathrooms, a new septic tank may be due. In Sanur, most older villas were built with simple concrete pits that degrade under tropical humidity and heavy monsoon rain. A modern biological treatment unit keeps the garden clean, protects groundwater, and cuts down on emergency call-outs every wet season.

When You Need a New Septic Tank

Several signs tell you the existing system has reached the end of its life. The most common in Sanur villas:

  • Persistent sewage smell around the garden, especially after heavy rain saturates the sandy soil.
  • Slow-draining toilets or showers that no amount of plunging fixes.
  • Visible pooling of grey water near the drain field during the November-to-March wet months.
  • You are adding a bathroom, kitchen sink, or laundry point and the current pit cannot handle the extra load.

Types of Septic Systems for a Bali Villa

There are broadly three options for a residential villa:

Conventional septic tanks (two-chamber concrete or plastic) rely on settling and partial anaerobic digestion. They are straightforward to install but need regular pumping and can still produce odour if overloaded.

Biological treatment units – often called biotech systems – use aerobic bacteria to break down organic matter more completely. They generate far less sludge, have a smaller footprint, and suit sandy coastal ground like Sanur's well. Many newer builds along Jl. Danau Poso and the beachfront lanes specify one of these.

A hybrid setup pairs a conventional holding tank with a small bio-filter or reed-bed. This is a sensible middle ground when your plot has room behind the house.

Whichever type you choose, confirm the unit is rated for tropical temperatures and that local spares are available. A technician who works around Sanur will know which models hold up best here.

Choosing the Right Volume

Volume is driven by how many people use the villa daily and how much water actually enters the system. In Sanur this matters because some households run entirely on PDAM mains while others supplement with a borehole or rooftop rainwater collection, changing total inflow significantly.

As a working rule, plan for roughly 150–200 litres of wastewater per person per day. A three-bedroom villa with four regular occupants therefore needs a tank in the 600–800 litre range before adding margin for guests. If you host regularly or a pool backwash drain feeds into the grey-water line, size up accordingly.

The technician can confirm the exact figure from photos of your current layout, number of fixtures, and typical occupancy – no need to guess.

The Installation Process Step by Step

A typical installation in a Sanur garden follows these stages:

  • Site survey: the technician walks the garden, checks soil (Sanur's sandy loam drains well but shifts under load), identifies existing drainage lines, and marks the dig area clear of tree roots and footpaths.
  • Excavation: a small excavator or manual crew digs the pit. Work is usually scheduled outside peak monsoon weeks so the open hole does not flood overnight.
  • Base preparation: a compacted sand-and-gravel bed keeps the tank level and prevents it floating in saturated ground.
  • Tank placement and connection: the unit is lowered in, inlet and outlet pipes are joined at the correct height, and an inspection chamber is set above ground for future access.
  • Backfilling and capping: surrounding soil is tamped back in layers, the top is sealed with concrete or a removable lid, and the surface is re-laid with pavers or grass.

Permits and Local Requirements

Replacing an existing septic tank in its original position generally does not require a full building permit, but you should inform your kelurahan office and, if applicable, your subak or compound HOA. Some beachfront compounds have their own drainage rules requiring outflow to connect to a communal soakaway rather than directly into the garden.

The technician can advise what documentation is typically expected for your specific address. Keep a note of the old tank's location – it helps if the kelurahan asks why the system is being replaced. If the new installation shifts even a few metres from the original spot, treat it as small civil works and check locally before digging.

Questions

How often does a biotech septic tank need servicing?

Most biological units need a sludge pump-out every 18 to 36 months depending on occupancy; conventional pits may need it more frequently. A Sanur-based technician can set up a simple reminder schedule after installation.

Can I install a septic tank during the rainy season?

It is possible but not ideal. Sandy soil in Sanur turns very soft from November to March, making excavation slower and risking the open pit flooding overnight. If timing allows, aim for the drier April-to-October window.

What should I do if I notice a sewage smell before the new tank is installed?

Stop using the affected toilet or shower, ventilate the area, and avoid pouring chemical drain cleaners into existing waste. Call your plumber to inspect before the smell spreads. Do not open sealed tanks yourself – hydrogen sulphide gas inside old pits can be dangerous.
