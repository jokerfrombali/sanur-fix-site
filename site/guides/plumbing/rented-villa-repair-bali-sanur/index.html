Rented Villa Plumbing Broke Down? Who Pays for Repairs

Plumbing fails at the worst moment, and when you are renting a villa in Sanur the first question is always whose bill this is. The short answer depends on what your lease actually says, but there is a practical process that works whether you have been here two months or two years. Here is how to handle it calmly, protect yourself, and get the job done properly.

What Is Usually Covered by Your Rental Agreement

Most long-term leases in Sanur split responsibility along a fairly standard line. Structural items — buried pipes, the septic tank, the water pump, rooftop storage tanks, and major fittings installed with the villa — fall under the owner or their agent. Consumables and small wear-and-tear parts like washers, O-rings, silicone seals, and aerators are typically the tenant's cost.

The problem is that many Sanur rental agreements are one-page documents written quickly, sometimes without a dedicated maintenance clause at all. Before you need anything fixed, scroll through your contract (or ask your agent) for three things: who handles plumbing repairs above a certain size, whether there is a response-time expectation, and whether you must use an approved contractor or can call anyone.

If the agreement is silent, the default assumption in most Bali rentals is that the owner covers anything that is not simple consumable replacement. But 'assumption' is not the same as 'agreement,' so clarify early.

How to Document the Breakdown Properly

Before you touch a wrench, take photos and a short video. Show the leak point, any standing water on the floor, and the general area so context is clear. Note the date and time on your phone screen in one of the shots if possible — it removes later arguments about when it started.

In older Sanur villas with concrete floors and open drainage channels, water damage spreads fast, especially during the rainy season from November to March. If water has reached electrical outlets, sockets, or the pump panel, stop using that circuit and do not attempt to dry it yourself. Call a professional before touching anything wet near electrics.

Keep a simple written log: what broke, when you noticed it, how much water escaped (a bucket, a basin, a puddle), and whether the issue is sudden (burst pipe) or gradual (slow drip over days). This distinction matters because a slow drip that you ignored for weeks can shift some responsibility toward the tenant.

  • Photograph the exact fitting or joint that failed
  • Record visible corrosion or mineral build-up (common with PDAM water and sea-air humidity)
  • Save the original packaging of any part you replaced yourself

Talking to Your Landlord or Agent Without Friction

Send your photos through WhatsApp first. A short message like 'Hi, there is a leak under the kitchen sink in Villa X — here are three photos. Could you confirm who should arrange the repair?' keeps things factual and puts everything in writing.

Avoid accusatory language. In Sanur's small rental community, relationships matter. If the owner has been responsive before, they will usually sort it within a day or two. If they have not replied after 48 hours, follow up once more politely and ask if they would prefer you to call a local technician and submit the invoice for reimbursement.

If the damage is urgent — active flooding, a burst main line, or sewage backing up from the septic system — do not wait for a reply. Call a plumber immediately, take extra photos while the work happens, and send the receipt to your landlord as soon as possible. Frame it as protecting their property, which it is.

Minor Repairs You Can Handle at Your Own Cost

A dripping tap caused by a worn washer, a loose hose connection on a washing machine, or a slow drain that clears with a plunger are all reasonable tenant-level fixes. These parts cost very little, and most landlords expect tenants to handle them without calling out a trade.

However, draw a clear line. Do not open a septic tank lid, dig around buried pipes, work on the pump wiring, or mix any cleaning chemicals near chlorine (pool area). In Sanur's salty air, fittings corrode faster than in mainland climates, so a joint that looks fine today can split next week. If you see green-blue corrosion or white salt crust on copper or brass, flag it to the owner rather than tightening it yourself.

If you do replace a small part, keep the old one and the receipt. It takes ten seconds and saves an argument later.

Calling a Technician and Getting a Proper Receipt

The fastest way to get a quote is to send clear photos of the problem on WhatsApp to a local plumber or pool technician who works in Sanur. A good technician will look at the fitting, the surrounding pipework, and any signs of corrosion, then give you a rough range before visiting. The final price depends on the job — access difficulty, whether parts need ordering from Denpasar, and how much water damage needs cleanup all factor in.

When the repair is done, ask for a written invoice or receipt showing: your name and villa address, date, description of work performed, parts used, and total amount paid. This document is what you hand to your landlord or agent for reimbursement if the repair falls under their responsibility.

For pool-related plumbing (backwash line, skimmer pump seals, chlorine dosing lines), the same process applies. Send photos of the leaking joint or the error light on the pump controller, get a quote, and collect the receipt. If the issue involves mixing chemicals near open water, stop and let the technician handle it.

Questions

Who pays if the septic tank overflows into the garden?

Septic tanks are structural infrastructure and almost always fall under the owner's responsibility. Call a licensed septic service immediately, photograph the overflow, and send the invoice to your landlord. Do not pour any chemicals into the tank yourself; that can void the owner's maintenance arrangement and create a health hazard.

Can I replace a dripping tap washer myself without asking the owner first?

Yes — small consumable replacements like washers, O-rings, and aerators are standard tenant-level maintenance in most Sanur leases. Just keep the old part and the shop receipt. If the tap body itself is corroded or cracked, that is a larger repair; photograph it and check with your agent before ordering a new tap.

What should I do if my landlord does not respond about a broken water pump?

Send one polite follow-up after 48 hours with your photos attached again. If there is still no reply and you have no running water, call a local technician, get the job done, and forward the receipt to your landlord with a note explaining the urgency. In Sanur, many villas rely on rooftop tanks fed by PDAM or a well pump, so a dead pump means no shower within a day or two.
