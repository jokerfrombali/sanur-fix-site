Pool Grout Repair in Sanur: When & How to Re-Grout Your Pool

Pool grout is the thin line of sealant between every tile in your villa's swimming pool. In Sanur's combination of chlorine, UV sun, and salty sea breeze, that sealant breaks down faster than you might expect. Once it crumbles or cracks, water seeps behind the tiles and can damage the plaster, the structure, and even the floor below. Here is what to look for, what to use, and how a proper re-grout job actually works.

Why Grout Fails Faster in Sanur

Sanur sits right on the coast, so the air carries fine salt particles that settle on every horizontal surface, including your pool walls. Add constant tropical UV exposure and high humidity, and the chemical bonds inside standard cement-based grout start to weaken within a couple of seasons.

Chlorine does its own share of damage from the inside out. Every time the filtration cycle runs, a low level of oxidiser washes over the joints. Over months this bleaches the colour, softens the material, and eventually causes it to flake away at the edges.

  • Salt-laden sea air accelerates surface erosion on exposed tile lines.
  • High humidity keeps grout slightly damp longer, encouraging micro-cracking.
  • Repeated chlorine contact degrades the binder faster than in temperate climates.

Epoxy vs Cement-Based Grout: What to Use

For a residential villa pool in Bali, epoxy grout is the go-to choice for most re-grouting jobs. It cures into a hard, non-porous surface that shrugs off chlorine, resists staining, and holds its colour even after years of sun. The trade-off is that it is more fiddly to apply and needs a clean, dry substrate before you mix it.

Cement-based (or 'polymer-modified') grout is easier to work with and costs less per bag, but in Sanur's climate it typically starts showing wear sooner. If your budget is tight or the pool is only used seasonally, a quality polymer-modified product can be acceptable, just plan to inspect it every rainy season.

  • Epoxy: best chemical resistance, longest life, harder to apply.
  • Polymer-modified cement: simpler application, shorter lifespan in tropical conditions.
  • Whichever type you choose, make sure it is rated for continuous water immersion.

Signs It Is Time to Re-Grout

Walk around the pool at eye level and run a finger along the tile lines. You are looking for softness, powdering, or visible gaps where the joint has opened up. Discolouration alone does not always mean failure, but if the grout crumbles under light pressure, the bond is gone.

A more serious warning sign is water staining on the plaster or concrete behind the tiles, especially near the waterline or at the pool floor. That means moisture is already getting past the grout and into the structure. In older Sanur villas with thin-shell pools, this can lead to leaks that are expensive to chase down later.

  • Grout feels powdery or flakes off when you scrape gently.
  • Joints have widened beyond 3 mm, letting water through.
  • White salt-like deposits appear on the tile face (sign of water cycling behind).
  • Plaster or render shows damp patches or delamination near the joints.

The Re-Grouting Process, Step by Step

First, the pool is drained to just below the lowest tile line so every joint is accessible and dry. The old grout is raked out with a narrow grout saw or an oscillating tool; in tight corners a hand scraper works better than power tools.

Each joint is then vacuumed and wiped with a damp cloth until no dust remains. For epoxy, the surface must be completely dry before mixing. The two-part epoxy is mixed according to the manufacturer's ratio, loaded into a rubber float or a caulking gun for wider gaps, and pressed firmly into each joint at a steady pace.

Excess is wiped away while still workable, following the product's open-time window. After that the pool needs a curing period before it can be refilled—usually 24 to 72 hours depending on the product and ambient humidity. In Bali's humid air, cure times can stretch toward the longer end, so plan accordingly.

  • Drain below waterline → remove old grout → clean joints thoroughly.
  • Mix and apply new grout within its working time.
  • Allow full cure (typically 1–3 days) before refilling.
  • Do not mix any chlorine-based cleaner with other chemicals during the process; if you smell strong fumes, ventilate immediately and call a professional.

How Long Does the Job Take?

For an average villa pool in Sanur—roughly 6 by 10 metres—the hands-on re-grouting usually takes one full day of skilled labour. Add the draining, cleaning, and curing stages and the pool is typically out of use for three to five calendar days from start to finish.

Larger or free-form pools, or ones where the plaster also needs patching, will take longer. The exact timeline depends on the size of the area, how much old material has to come out, and the weather that week. A technician can give you a realistic schedule after seeing photos of your specific pool.

Questions

Can I just paint over cracked grout instead of replacing it?

No. Paint or sealant applied over crumbling grout does not restore the structural bond between tiles. Water will still find its way behind, and the coating itself will peel within a season in Bali's climate. Proper removal and replacement is the only durable fix.

Do I need to drain the whole pool to re-grout the walls?

You do not need to empty the pool completely. Draining to just below the lowest tile line (usually around 15–20 cm) gives full access to every joint while keeping the structure stable and reducing the time the pool is offline.

How often should I check my pool grout in Sanur?

A quick visual walk-around once a month is enough for most villa owners. Pay extra attention after the rainy season, when humidity and occasional flooding can hide early cracking. If you notice any softness or widening, get it looked at before it spreads to adjacent joints.
