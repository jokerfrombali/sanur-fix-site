Pool Pump Not Working? How It Works & What Goes Wrong

Your pool pump is the heart of the whole system – it pulls water through the filter, pushes it back into the pool, and keeps everything circulating. In Sanur's salty air and constant heat, pumps work harder than they would in a temperate climate, so small issues can become big ones fast. Here is what is actually inside that box by your pool, why it starts humming or grinding, and how to tell when it needs a repair versus a full replacement.

How Your Pool Pump Actually Works

Strip away the plastic housing and you will find three main parts: an electric motor, a centrifugal impeller (a small fan-like disc), and a strainer basket that catches leaves and debris before they reach the impeller. The motor spins the impeller at high speed, creating suction on the inlet side and pressure on the outlet side. Water flows in, gets flung outward, and is pushed through your sand or cartridge filter and back into the pool.

Around the impeller shaft sits a mechanical seal – a thin rubber-and-ceramic ring that stops water from leaking into the motor windings. This is the single most failure-prone component in any pump, especially here in Bali where PDAM tap water or well water carries dissolved minerals that slowly eat away at rubber seals over time.

  • Strainer basket – catch-all for leaves, insects, and sand; check it weekly during dry season, daily during rainy season
  • Impeller – spins to create flow; made of plastic or bronze, can chip or corrode
  • Mechanical seal – keeps water out of the motor; wears with every hour of operation
  • Motor windings and capacitor – provide the electrical torque; sensitive to humidity and salt-laden air

The Breakdowns I See Most Often in Sanur Villas

After years servicing pumps across Sanur – from newer beachfront villas to older compounds near the river mouth – the same handful of failures comes up again and again. The mechanical seal weeping a slow drip onto the floor is number one. You will notice a small puddle under the pump or a faint mineral ring on the base plate.

Second is a worn impeller. Mineral deposits from well water build up on the vanes, reducing flow until the pool barely circulates. Third is a failed start capacitor: the motor hums but does not spin. Fourth, and more serious, is corrosion of the motor housing itself. Sanur's sea breeze carries fine salt particles that settle on metal parts, especially if the pump sits open-air without a cover.

Why Your Pump Is Humming, Grinding, or Squealing

A steady low hum while running is normal. What is not normal is a rising whine, a rhythmic grinding, or a squeal that changes pitch as the speed varies. A grinding sound almost always means the bearings inside the motor are wearing out. Once you hear it, the bearing can seize within days, taking the whole motor with it.

A gurgling or cavitation noise – like water boiling at low temperature – usually points to an air lock in the suction line or a clogged strainer basket. During Bali's rainy season, a single large leaf can block the inlet and cause this instantly. Open the basket lid, clear the debris, and restart. If the gurgle persists, check for a cracked suction pipe or a loose union joint letting air in.

If the pump makes a loud buzzing but the impeller does not turn, do not keep switching it on. That is the capacitor struggling against a seized shaft. Switch it off at the breaker and call a technician before you burn out the windings.

Overheating: The Silent Killer in Tropical Heat

Sanur sits at roughly 30 °C year-round, and pool equipment areas often get little airflow. Pumps run continuously for 8–12 hours a day, so heat builds up faster than manufacturers who design for cooler climates expect. A blocked intake screen, a partially closed valve, or running the pump dry (for example after backwashing with the filter valve set wrong) will push the motor past its safe temperature within minutes.

Most modern pumps have a thermal overload protector that trips the motor if it gets too hot. You will hear a click, then silence. Let it cool for 15–20 minutes before restarting. If it trips again immediately, stop using it. Repeated overheating degrades the insulation on the windings and can cause an electrical short – which, near standing water, is a genuine shock hazard. In that case, switch the circuit off at the panel and do not touch the pump until a qualified electrician or plumber has inspected it.

Repair It or Replace It? A Practical Way to Decide

A seal replacement or impeller swap is usually straightforward and keeps a good pump going for another two or three years. If your pump is under five years old, the housing is solid, and only one component has failed, repairing it almost always makes sense. The exact cost depends on the model and what needs replacing; I quote from photos you send me on WhatsApp before any work starts.

If the motor itself is burnt out, the housing is pitted with corrosion, or you are already on your second repair in a year, a new pump may be the wiser choice. Older villas sometimes have pumps that were never properly grounded or installed with undersized pipework; swapping the unit gives you a chance to fix those underlying issues at the same time.

Whichever route you take, make sure the new or repaired pump sits on a level, ventilated base, ideally under a small roof overhang to keep direct sun and salt spray off the electrical connections.

Questions

My pool pump hums but no water comes through the filter. What should I check first?

Open the strainer basket and clear any debris. Then check that the suction pipe union is tight and that the filter valve is set to 'Filter' rather than 'Backwash' or 'Closed'. If the impeller still does not spin after clearing the basket, switch the pump off at the breaker – a seized shaft or dead capacitor needs a technician's look.

How often should I service my pool pump in Sanur?

A quick visual check of the seal area, strainer basket, and motor housing every month keeps problems visible before they grow. A full internal inspection (seal, bearings, impeller) once a year is sensible given the humidity and mineral content of local water. During the rainy season, add an extra mid-season check because leaves and insects clog intakes faster.

Can I just mix chlorine tablets into the pump basket to save time?

No. Chlorine tablets are designed for dedicated feeders or floating dispensers, not the strainer basket. Dissolving them there releases concentrated acid directly onto the impeller and seals, corroding metal parts within weeks. Never combine different chlorine products or add chlorine while the pump is running dry – the fumes can be dangerous. If you are unsure about your dosing setup, ask me to walk you through it on site.
