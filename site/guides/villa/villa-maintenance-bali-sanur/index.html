Villa Maintenance in Bali: What's Actually Included

If you own or rent a villa in Sanur, "maintenance" can mean anything from topping up chlorine to pumping out a full septic tank. In practice, regular upkeep covers your pool, water supply, drainage, and the systems that quietly keep the house running through both wet and dry seasons. Here is what a typical maintenance visit actually includes, so you know what to expect before you book one.

The Systems That Need Looking After

A typical Sanur villa has more hidden infrastructure than most people realise when they first move in. The pool pump, filter, and chemical dosing system are obvious, but underneath them sit a water supply line (PDAM mains or a private well), a rooftop storage tank, a set of drains and overflow pipes, and usually a septic tank with its soakaway field. Older villas built in the 1990s often have galvanized fittings that corrode faster because of the salt-laden sea air rolling off the beach.

None of these components announce themselves until something leaks, clogs, or smells. A scheduled check-up catches small issues — a weeping joint, a slow drain, a tank lid lifting in the wind — while they are still cheap and quick to fix.

  • Pool: pump, filter cartridge/sand, skimmer basket, tile grout, chlorine/pH balance
  • Water supply: inlet valve, pressure gauge, rooftop tank float and lid seal
  • Drainage: floor drains, gully traps, overflow from the tank, downpipes
  • Septic tank: liquid level, vent pipe, no foul smell at the soakaway
  • Fittings and joints: visible corrosion on exposed metal, especially near the pool deck

A Realistic Maintenance Schedule

You do not need a technician every week, but you do need a rhythm. The table below is what most Sanur villa owners settle into after the first rainy season teaches them a lesson.

  • Weekly (you or your housekeeper): skim leaves off the pool, top up water, check pH strip, clear the skimmer basket
  • Monthly (technician visit): test and adjust pool chemistry, inspect pump seals and filter, flush the rooftop tank, check all visible plumbing for drips, clean drain covers
  • Before the rainy season (around October–November): clear gutters and downpipes, verify the septic tank has room before heavy rain dilutes it, tighten loose roof sheets, confirm the tank lid locks properly
  • Every 6–12 months: backwash or replace the pool filter media, inspect well pump if applicable, have the septic tank level checked and pumped out if needed
  • Annually: full tile and grout inspection around the pool, pressure-test exposed pipes, review electrical junction boxes near wet areas (electrician's job — see safety note below)

Who Actually Does the Work

A private plumber-and-pool technician in Sanur can handle the pool system end-to-end, all domestic plumbing, the rooftop tank, drainage, and basic septic-tank checks. That is one person, one phone number, no call-centre queue.

What falls outside that scope: structural roofing, major electrical rewiring, gas appliance servicing, and any work where water and electricity meet at a junction box. If you smell gas anywhere in the villa, stop what you are doing, open windows, and call a licensed gas fitter immediately. Do not flip switches or use your phone inside the affected area until the source is confirmed safe.

One more safety rule worth repeating: never mix chlorine-based products with acid or ammonia-based cleaners. The resulting gas can injure you within seconds. If you are unsure which product to add to the pool, put the bottle down and ask before pouring.

How Much It Costs

There is no fixed "villa maintenance" price tag in Bali because every property is different — a two-bedroom beachfront villa with a well and a 10-metre lap pool needs very different attention from a single-key rental with PDAM supply and a small plunge pool.

The practical way to get a number: take photos of the areas you want checked (pool equipment, tank, visible pipes, septic vent) and send them on WhatsApp. A technician will look at the photos, ask a couple of questions about age and usage, and give you a quote for that specific job. You approve it before any work starts, so there are no surprises on the invoice.

Putting It in Writing

Even if you prefer to keep things informal, a short written agreement protects both sides. It does not need to be a legal contract; a one-page summary shared over WhatsApp works fine for most Sanur owners.

What to include: which systems are covered, how often visits happen (monthly, bi-monthly), what is explicitly excluded (e.g., major pump replacement, electrical work), who supplies chemicals or spare parts, and how urgent call-outs are handled. Agree on a response window for leaks or pool shutdowns so expectations are clear during the rainy season when everything gets busier.

  • Scope: list each system by name, not just "plumbing"
  • Frequency and access: day-of-week, time slot, who lets the tech in
  • Exclusions: structural repairs, gas, electrical, landscaping
  • Parts and chemicals: who buys them, whether they are billed at cost
  • Communication channel: WhatsApp number, preferred hours

Questions

Do I really need monthly maintenance if my villa looks fine?

In Sanur's humidity and salt air, small corrosion and algae growth progress faster than you notice. A monthly check usually takes under an hour and catches a weeping joint or a drifting pH level before it becomes a tile-replacement job or a pump failure.

Can one person handle both the pool and the plumbing?

Yes — that is exactly what a combined plumber-and-pool technician does. The pool pump, filter, and chemical system share the same water-supply line as your taps and tank, so having one set of eyes on all of it avoids finger-pointing when something drips.

What should I do if I find a leak while the technician is not on site?

Close the relevant isolation valve (usually near the meter for supply leaks, or the pool pump switch for pool-side leaks), take photos and a short video showing the wet area, and send them on WhatsApp with a note about when you first noticed it. Do not attempt to open a septic tank lid yourself; call the technician out.
