Villa Maintenance Costs in Sanur: What to Budget Each Year

Owning or renting a villa in Sanur means living with salt air, monsoon downpours, and plumbing that was never designed for tropical humidity. Your yearly budget should cover pool chemistry, water supply, routine plumbing, and a buffer for the things you cannot predict. Here is where the money actually goes and what to watch each season.

Where Your Villa Budget Actually Goes

Before you open your spreadsheet, it helps to know the five buckets every Sanur villa owner deals with: pool upkeep, water (PDAM, well, rooftop tank), plumbing and septic, structural fixes, and garden or drainage. The exact amount shifts depending on whether you have a two-bedroom rental unit or a four-bedroom compound with a private well, but the categories stay the same.

A useful habit is to log every small expense — a bag of chlorine, a plumber's call-out, a new gasket — in one place. After six months you will see which line items are recurring and which are one-off surprises. That pattern tells you how much to set aside monthly rather than scrambling when something breaks.

  • Pool chemicals, pump parts, tile grout
  • Water bills (PDAM) plus any well-pumping electricity
  • Septic tank pumping and drain-line clearing
  • Roof, gutter, and slab-leak repairs
  • Garden drainage after heavy monsoon rain

Pool Maintenance in Sanur's Climate

Sanur sits right on the coast, so salt-laden air settles on ladders, handrails, and the pump housing within weeks if they are not rinsed. In the dry season the heat drives evaporation fast, meaning your chemical balance drifts more often. In the rainy season, sudden downpours dilute chlorine and push organic matter into the water, so you may need to shock-treat twice as often between October and March.

Check the filter pressure gauge weekly. If it reads higher than normal, backwash or clean the cartridge before the pump works harder than it should. Inspect the skimmer basket daily during pollen-heavy mornings; clogged baskets cause the pump to cavitate and shorten its life.

One safety note: never mix different pool chemicals together, especially anything containing acid with chlorine-based products. The reaction can release toxic gas. If you smell a sharp, irritating fume near the pool equipment, step away from the area, ventilate, and call a professional before touching anything.

  • Rinse metal fittings with fresh water every two weeks (salt corrosion)
  • Test free chlorine and pH at least three times a week in dry season
  • Shock-treat after heavy rain or a full week of bather load
  • Inspect the return jets for cracks where salt has eaten the grout

Water: PDAM, Wells, and Rooftop Tanks

Most Sanur villas run on PDAM piped water stored in a rooftop tank, but older properties — and some newer builds on the eastern side of the peninsula — also tap a bore well. PDAM pressure in Sanur is not consistent; you may get a strong flow one morning and a trickle the next. That is why the rooftop tank matters: it smooths out the supply so your shower does not stop mid-wash.

Clean the tank at least once a year. Sediment, algae, and small debris settle at the bottom and can clog the inlet valve or give the water an off taste. Check the overflow pipe to make sure it drains clear of the wall and that the float valve still shuts off correctly.

If your villa uses a well, test the water periodically for iron and hardness. High iron stains fixtures orange and accelerates scale inside pipes. A septic system feeding into the ground needs pumping roughly every two to three years depending on occupancy; ignore that schedule and you will smell it before you see the backup.

  • Flush the rooftop tank annually; replace the inlet screen if cracked
  • Check PDAM meter readings monthly to catch silent leaks early
  • Have well water tested for iron, pH, and total dissolved solids yearly
  • Schedule septic pumping based on number of occupants, not just time

Repairs You Can't Postpone

The most expensive plumbing job in Bali is almost always the one that was ignored for months. A slow leak under a bathroom slab feeds moisture into the concrete, softens the subfloor, and eventually corrodes the copper or PEX line below. By the time you lift the tile, the repair is no longer a gasket swap — it is a re-pipe and re-tile.

Roof work should be done before the rainy season, not during it. In Sanur the first hard downpour can reveal a crack in a clay tile or a split in a gutter joint that has been widening all dry season. Walk the roof (or have someone do it) in April or May and seal any gaps while the sky is still clear.

Two situations where you stop what you are doing and call a professional immediately: a strong gas smell near the kitchen or generator area, and any electrical fault within arm's reach of standing water. Do not flip breakers repeatedly, do not open a gas valve to 'test' the line. Step outside, ventilate, and wait for a licensed technician.

  • Fix slab leaks early; moisture damage compounds weekly in humidity
  • Seal roof and gutter joints before October rains begin
  • Replace corroded pipe sections rather than patching them repeatedly
  • Keep the septic vent pipe unobstructed so gases escape safely

Setting Aside a Reserve Fund

A practical approach: set aside a fixed amount each month into a separate account labelled 'villa maintenance.' The figure depends on your villa's size, age, and whether you run a pool — there is no universal number. What matters is consistency, so that when the pump motor dies in February or the septic needs an emergency pump-out, you are not choosing between the repair and this month's groceries.

When you need a quote, take two or three clear photos of the problem area, note the dimensions if relevant, and send them over WhatsApp. A good technician will look at the images, ask a couple of follow-up questions, and give you a range before anyone drives out. That keeps small jobs from becoming expensive call-outs and gives you time to compare options.

  • Review your logged expenses every six months and adjust the monthly set-aside
  • Budget extra for the first year in a new villa (unknown wear-and-tear items)
  • Keep contact details for one trusted plumber/pool tech and one electrician handy

Questions

How often should I have my septic tank pumped in Sanur?

As a general guideline, every two to three years for a family of four. If you host guests regularly or run a rental with high turnover, lean toward the shorter end. Watch for slow drains, gurgling sounds, or a wet patch near the soakaway as early warning signs that it is due sooner.

Is it normal for PDAM water pressure to drop in Sanur?

Yes. Pressure fluctuates by neighbourhood and time of day, especially during peak morning hours when many villas fill their tanks simultaneously. If your flow drops to almost nothing for more than an hour, check your rooftop tank level and inlet valve first; if those are fine, it may be a main-line issue worth reporting to PDAM.

What should I inspect before the rainy season starts?

Walk the roof and gutters, clear leaves from downpipes, test the pool pump under load, flush the rooftop tank, and confirm the septic vent pipe is unobstructed. A thirty-minute checklist in April saves you from emergency calls in November when the sky opens up for days at a time.
