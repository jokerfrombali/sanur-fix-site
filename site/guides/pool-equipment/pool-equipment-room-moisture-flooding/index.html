Pool Equipment Room: Moisture & Flooding Fixes for Sanur Villas

Bali's year-round humidity plus a few monsoon downpours can turn a small pool pump room into a corrosion trap within months. The good news is that most recurring problems trace back to three fixable gaps: airflow, floor drainage, and basic protection of electrical gear. Here is what to check and what to change before the next wet season hits.

What Goes Wrong in a Wet Pump Room

Walk into your pump room after a rainy week and you will probably notice damp concrete, a film of water on the pipe joints, and maybe a faint metallic smell from oxidising fittings. In Sanur the sea breeze adds salt particles to the mix, so even a thin layer of condensation accelerates rust on stainless-steel unions and aluminium heat-exchanger fins.

Older villas built before 2010 often have no dedicated exhaust fan, a flat concrete floor with zero slope, and an overflow line from the rooftop tank that simply drips onto the pump skid. Well-water supply lines are another common leak source because the higher mineral content eats through rubber washers faster than PDAM mains water does.

  • Condensation pooling around the filter housing and pressure gauge
  • Corroded or pitted pipe threads, especially on well-water inlet
  • Damp or discoloured paint on the electrical distribution board
  • Mould streaks on ceiling tiles or exposed rebar

Ventilation That Actually Works in the Tropics

A sealed pump room in Bali is asking for trouble. You need at least two air paths: a low-level intake near the floor (behind a mesh screen to keep out insects) and an exhaust outlet high on the opposite wall. A small axial fan rated for continuous duty, wired to a humidity sensor set around 75 % RH, will pull warm moist air out before it settles on cold metal surfaces.

If your villa sits within a few hundred metres of Sanur beach, add a fine-mesh pre-filter to the intake so salt-laden air does not coat every component. Run the fan for ten minutes after each pool cycle; the heat generated by the pump and heater creates a natural updraft that the fan can exploit.

  • Install an exhaust fan with a built-in thermostat or humidity switch
  • Keep the intake vent at least 15 cm above the finished floor level
  • Do not seal the room completely – even a 20 × 20 cm louvered panel helps
  • In the rainy season, run the fan continuously if indoor humidity stays above 80 %

Drainage: Your First Line of Defence Against Flooding

The single most important detail is slope. The concrete floor should fall at roughly 1–2 % toward a floor drain positioned in the lowest corner, ideally directly beneath the filter and pump skid. If your villa's floor is dead flat, water from a leaking union or an overflowing tank will simply spread and soak into electrical connections.

Check the rooftop-tank overflow pipe during the first heavy rain of the wet season (usually November). In many older Sanur villas that pipe discharges inside the pump room or against the wall next to it. Reroute it to an external storm-water channel or connect it to the garden drainage line. A small submersible sump pump sitting in the floor drain, triggered by a float switch, gives you a safety net for the nights when a valve fails while everyone is asleep.

  • Verify the floor falls toward the drain; add a thin screep layer if needed
  • Route the rooftop-tank overflow outside the building envelope
  • Fit a battery-backup sump pump in the floor drain as a second layer
  • Inspect well-water supply valves and PDAM isolation taps quarterly for weeping

Protecting Pumps, Heaters & Electrical Panels

Elevate every piece of equipment on rubber feet or a short steel frame so the base sits at least 5 cm above the finished floor. This keeps the motor windings and control boards out of standing water even after a minor leak. The electrical distribution board should be in a sealed IP65 enclosure with a gasketed door, mounted high on the wall – never below 1 m from the floor.

Store chlorine tablets and any acid dosing chemicals on a separate shelf away from the heater and pump. If you ever smell gas near the pool area, shut off the supply immediately, ventilate the space, and call a licensed technician before touching anything. Never mix chlorine-based products with other cleaning agents; the resulting vapour can damage both your lungs and the metal fittings around it.

  • Mount the DB board in an IP65 box, minimum 1 m above floor level
  • Keep chemical storage at least 1 m from heaters and electrical gear
  • Use dielectric unions between dissimilar metals to slow galvanic corrosion
  • Fit anti-condensation tape or a small desiccant pack inside the heater cabinet

A 10-Minute Monthly Walkthrough

Set a phone reminder for the same day each month. Open the pump room, run the pool cycle for five minutes, then walk through this quick list while the system is under pressure. You are looking for new drips, fresh rust streaks, a musty smell that was not there last month, or water pooling in a corner that used to stay dry.

If you spot a leaking union, a corroded fitting, or water behind the filter housing, photograph it clearly (wide shot plus close-up) and send it over for a quote. A technician can usually tell from two or three photos whether it is a washer swap, a pipe section replacement, or something structural that needs on-site assessment.

  • Check every visible joint and the filter lid gasket for weeping
  • Look under the pump skid for hidden puddles
  • Wipe a finger along pipe threads; white/green residue means active corrosion
  • Confirm the exhaust fan actually switches on when humidity rises
  • Note any new damp patches on walls or ceiling and track them week to week

Questions

How often should I inspect the pool equipment room?

A visual check once a month is enough for most Sanur villas. Bump it up to twice a month during the November-to-April rainy season, and do a full walkthrough after any storm that dumps more than an hour of heavy rain. The goal is to catch a slow drip before it becomes a flooded floor.

My pump room flooded overnight – what should I do first?

Switch off the power at the main breaker before stepping in. Do not restart the pump or heater until you have confirmed no water reached the motor windings or control board. Mop out standing water, open all windows, run the exhaust fan, and photograph the damage. If you see water inside the electrical panel or smell burnt insulation, leave everything off and call a professional to assess before re-energising.

Is condensation on pipes normal in Bali's humidity?

A thin film of moisture on cold-water supply lines is common in tropical climates and usually evaporates within an hour. What is not normal is persistent pooling, dripping from joints, or white/green mineral deposits building up over weeks. That pattern points to a leak or a ventilation gap rather than ambient humidity alone.
