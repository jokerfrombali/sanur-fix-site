How to Unclog a Clogged Toilet Without Breaking It

A clogged toilet is stressful, but in most cases you can clear it yourself with a proper plunger or a closet auger before anything cracks or overflows. The key is working gently, using the right tool for the job, and knowing when the problem is actually downstream in your septic tank rather than in the bowl itself. Here is a step-by-step approach that works well in older Sanur villas where the plumbing was not always built for heavy paper loads.

Why Toilet Paper Is a Problem in Bali

In many Western countries the sewer network is wide enough to handle a few sheets of tissue. In Bali, especially in the older two-storey villas around Sanur, the internal pipes are often narrower, and the on-site septic system was designed assuming residents use a small waste bin next to the toilet. Flush a roll's worth of paper and you have created a plug that sits right at the trap bend.

Add the local water situation into the mix. Some villas run off PDAM mains, others off a private well feeding a rooftop tank. Pressure fluctuates between the dry and rainy seasons, so a partial blockage that barely notices in July can become a full backup by November when every shower and washing machine adds volume to the same small tank.

Using a Plunger the Right Way

Skip the flat rubber cup you find in most hotel bathrooms. You want a flange plunger — the one with a fold-down rubber flap that seals around the round drain hole rather than the rim of the bowl. If your villa only has the flat type, it will still work but you need more force and more patience.

Make sure there is enough water in the bowl to cover the base of the plunger (about two inches). Press down gently first to seat the seal, then pull up firmly while keeping the cup submerged. Push and pull ten to fifteen times without breaking the suction. If the water level drops and swirls, you are through. Rinse the bowl and check the flush.

One warning for older Sanur villas: many were built in the 1980s or 90s with a two-piece ceramic toilet where the bowl sits on a separate base held by bolts. Yanking the plunger out violently can crack the joint or chip the glaze. Pull steadily, never jerk.

The Plumbing Snake (Closet Auger)

If the plunger does not clear it, grab a closet auger — sometimes called a toilet snake. This is different from the generic drain snake used for sinks; a closet auger has a longer, stiffer cable and a curved tip that follows the S-bend of the trap without scraping the porcelain.

Insert the tip into the drain hole, push until you feel resistance, then turn the handle clockwise while applying gentle forward pressure. Work the cable in and out a few centimetres at a time. You will usually feel it catch on paper or a small object within thirty seconds to a couple of minutes. Back it out slowly so anything caught comes with the cable.

If you hit solid, unyielding resistance more than ten centimetres past the rim, stop. You may be pushing against the wax ring or the pipe wall, and forcing further can damage both. At that point it is safer to call someone rather than keep cranking.

When the Clog Is Actually in the Septic Tank

A single blocked bowl is one thing. If your kitchen sink drains slowly at the same time, if you hear gurgling from the shower when you flush, or if there is a faint sewage smell near the tank area behind the garden, the blockage is probably not in the toilet at all — it is downstream, in the drain line or inside the septic chamber itself.

In Sanur many older villas have relatively small concrete tanks that were sized for a family of four using a bin for waste. Once an expat household starts flushing paper regularly, the sludge layer builds up faster and the outlet pipe chokes. The rainy season makes this worse because surface water infiltrates the tank through hairline cracks, diluting the system and pushing solids into the leach field.

Do not open the tank lid yourself. Hydrogen sulphide gas collects in the headspace and can knock you out within seconds. This is a job for a licensed pump-out service or a plumber who knows where the access points are on your plot. If multiple fixtures are affected, stop using all water outlets and call a professional before the overflow reaches the pool or the street.

Preventing Trouble From Guests

The single most effective step is a small, polite sign next to the toilet: 'Please place used tissue in the bin – thank you.' Keep it in English and Bahasa Indonesia so housekeepers and visiting friends both understand. Pair it with a small lidded bin lined with a plastic bag; empty it daily as part of the cleaning routine.

Before any guest arrives, do a quick flush test and make sure the plunger is stored somewhere obvious — under the sink cabinet works well. Remind guests verbally that wet wipes, cotton pads, and sanitary products go in the bin, never the bowl. It sounds basic, but one forgotten pack of 'flushable' wipes has caused more septic emergencies in Sanur villas than anything else I see.

  • Sign by the toilet (English + Indonesian)
  • Lined waste bin emptied daily
  • Flush-test before every guest check-in
  • Plunger kept accessible, not locked away

Questions

Can I pour boiling water down a clogged toilet?

No. The sudden temperature change can crack old ceramic bowls, especially the two-piece types common in older Sanur villas. If you need to add water for the plunger, use lukewarm tap water from your rooftop tank or well supply. Avoid hot water entirely near the porcelain.

How do I tell if it is the toilet or the septic tank?

If only the toilet is affected and other fixtures drain normally, the blockage is almost certainly in the trap or the short pipe behind the wall — try the plunger and auger first. If the kitchen sink, shower, or outdoor tap are also slow or gurgling at the same time, the problem is further downstream in the drain line or the septic chamber, and that needs a professional inspection.

What should I never put in my villa toilet in Bali?

Wet wipes (even ones labelled 'flushable'), paper towels, cotton pads, sanitary products, and large amounts of standard tissue. In a small on-site septic system these items do not break down the way they would in a municipal sewer. Use the bin next to the toilet for everything except a small amount of thin tissue, and even then keep it minimal.
