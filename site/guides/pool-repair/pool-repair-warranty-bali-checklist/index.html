A pool repair warranty in Bali usually covers only the specific fault that was fixed, not every crack or leak that appears months later. In Sanur's salty sea air and monsoon downpours, pools take extra punishment, so knowing exactly what is guaranteed matters more than the sticker price. Run through this checklist before any technician starts cutting into your tile or plumbing.

What Your Pool Repair Warranty Actually Covers

Most pool repairs in Sanur come with a workmanship guarantee on the component that was touched: a replaced pump seal, a re-grouted tile section, a new plumbing joint under the deck. The materials themselves may carry a separate manufacturer period, but that is different from the contractor's promise that their installation will hold.

What typically falls outside the warranty: damage caused by a storm surge during the rainy season, chemical misuse (especially mixing chlorine with acidic products), structural settling of an older villa foundation, or a new pipe break somewhere else in the system. If your pool sits near the coast and salt spray accelerates corrosion on metal fittings, ask explicitly whether those parts are included.

  • Covered: the repaired part, the workmanship around it, and usually one free follow-up visit within the stated window.
  • Not covered: pre-existing cracks you did not disclose, damage from heavy rain flooding an unsealed equipment room, or wear-and-tear on gaskets and O-rings.
  • Ask in writing which water source applies to your setup – PDAM mains or a well – because mineral content changes how fast seals degrade.

Documents You Should Collect Before Work Starts

A verbal agreement over WhatsApp is convenient, but it is hard to enforce when a leak reappears three weeks later. Before anyone lifts a tile, make sure you have a short written scope: what will be opened, what parts will be replaced, what materials are specified, and how long the warranty lasts in months.

In older Sanur villas the plumbing under the pool deck was sometimes modified by previous owners without any record. Ask the technician to note anything unusual they find during the job and add it to the document. If the repair touches electrical components near the water line, confirm that the person doing the work is qualified for wet-area electrics; if they are not, stop and call a licensed electrician before proceeding.

  • Written scope of work with dates and signatures (a photo of both parties' hands holding the paper works fine).
  • Parts list with material type, so you can verify replacements later.
  • Warranty duration stated clearly – 30 days, 90 days, six months – and what triggers its start date.
  • Contact details for follow-up visits and who to message if the issue returns.

Before-and-After Photos: Your Best Protection

Take your own photos before the contractor arrives and again when they finish. Include a fixed reference object in frame – a tile pattern, a drain cover, a corner of the coping stone – so the angle is consistent. Add a timestamp or place a newspaper with the date next to the area being worked on.

During the rainy season, algae blooms and mineral stains appear quickly after heavy rain. Without dated photos it becomes very hard to prove whether a discolouration existed before the repair or appeared after. Ask the technician to send you their own set of images through WhatsApp; this also gives you a record of exactly which tiles were lifted and re-set.

When Things Go Wrong: Handling Disputes

If a leak returns within the warranty window, contact the same person who did the job first. Send clear photos and a short description of what you see – dripping from a specific joint, water pooling under the deck, a change in pump pressure. Most honest technicians will arrange a follow-up visit without drama.

If the response is slow or the explanation does not match what you are seeing, keep everything in writing. Do not attempt to 'test' the fix by running the pool at unusual chemical levels; mixing chlorine with any other product can create toxic gas, so stop immediately if you smell something sharp and ventilate the area. For electrical faults near the pool, switch off the breaker and wait for a qualified professional rather than opening panels yourself.

As a last resort, a neutral third-party inspection (another local plumber or pool tech) can confirm whether the original fault was actually addressed. The cost of that check depends on the scope, and the technician will quote after seeing your photos on WhatsApp.

How to Choose a Pool Contractor in Sanur

You do not need a large company; you need someone who knows the specific quirks of Sanur pools: the salt-laden air that eats stainless fittings, the way PDAM water differs from well water in hardness, and the older villa layouts where the septic tank sits close to the pool's underground drain line.

A practical first step is to send three or four clear photos of the problem through WhatsApp and ask for an assessment before anyone drives out. A good contractor will tell you what they think the issue is, what parts are likely involved, and give you a price range once they have seen enough detail. If the answer is vague or pushes you into a full renovation without inspecting first, keep looking.

Ask whether they handle both plumbing and tile work under one roof, so there is no finger-pointing if a re-grouted section starts weeping because the pipe behind it was never properly sealed.

Questions

How long should a pool repair warranty last in Bali?

There is no single legal standard. For seal replacements and small plumbing joints, 30 to 90 days is common. For larger jobs like re-tile sections or pump swaps, six months or more is reasonable. Whatever period is agreed, make sure it is written down with a start date tied to completion, not to the day the job was booked.

Does the warranty still apply if I use well water instead of PDAM?

The water source can affect how quickly seals and gaskets degrade, but it does not automatically void a workmanship guarantee on a specific repair. That said, disclose your water source before the job so the technician can choose appropriate materials. If a part fails unusually fast because of high mineral content, that may fall outside the contractor's responsibility.

What do I do if the same leak comes back after the warranty period ends?

You will likely need to pay for the visit again, but you are not starting from zero. Bring your original photos, the parts list, and any follow-up messages. A competent technician can often diagnose whether the failure is related to the earlier repair (which might still be addressed as good faith) or a genuinely new issue elsewhere in the system.
