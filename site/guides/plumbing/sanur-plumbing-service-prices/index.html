A plumbing job in Sanur isn't one fixed price – it depends on what's broken, where your villa sits, and whether parts need sourcing locally. The technician will usually look at photos first, give you a range, and confirm once they're on site. Here's how the numbers actually add up so you know what to expect when you pick up the phone.

What Makes Up the Price

Every quote has three moving pieces: labour, travel, and materials. In Sanur that means the plumber drives through traffic from their workshop to your street, spends time diagnosing the issue, then either fixes it with what's in the van or orders a part from a hardware shop in Tuban or Legian.

Older villas near the beach often have corroded copper pipes or cracked septic-tank connections, which adds complexity compared to a newer build. The salt-laden sea air also accelerates corrosion on exposed fittings, so a simple tap replacement can turn into a small pipe-section swap. None of this is hidden cost – it's just why two identical-looking jobs can end up at different prices.

Call-Out and Diagnosis

When you call, the first thing the technician needs is context: what's leaking, where, and since when. A quick photo or short video sent over WhatsApp saves everyone time. If the problem is obvious – a dripping showerhead, a blocked sink drain – the quote can be given before the van even leaves.

For less visible issues like a slow-draining toilet, a pressure drop across multiple taps, or a suspected crack in an underground PDAM supply line, the technician will charge for the visit itself because diagnosis takes real time. That diagnostic fee usually counts toward the final repair bill if you go ahead with the job. It's standard practice and protects both sides.

Materials and Parts

Parts make up a variable chunk of any invoice. A washer or O-ring costs very little; a full-length PPR pipe run, a new septic-tank inlet valve, or a rooftop tank float mechanism costs more. In Sanur most common fittings are available locally within a day, but specialised items – certain pump models, specific pool chemical dosing parts, or imported brass components – may need ordering and that adds lead time.

The technician should itemise parts separately from labour so you can see exactly what you're paying for. If a part isn't in stock, ask whether they'll source it themselves or prefer you to buy it at a local hardware store. Either way works, just confirm who handles the purchase before work begins.

Emergency and After-Hours Calls

A burst pipe at 2 a.m. during the rainy season is not the same as a scheduled tap swap on a Tuesday morning. Emergency calls – flooding, sewage backup into the villa, a gas smell near the water heater – carry a higher rate because the technician leaves other work to come to you immediately.

If you smell gas or notice electrics sparking near a wet area, stop using the appliance, shut off the supply if you safely can, and call a professional right away. Don't mix chlorine with any other cleaning product while waiting; the fumes are genuinely dangerous. For non-urgent issues, booking ahead almost always costs less than an after-hours emergency visit.

How to Avoid Overpaying

Three simple habits keep your bill honest. First, send clear photos before the visit: the leaking joint, the model number on the pump, the brand of the fixture. The more detail you give, the tighter the quote.

Second, ask for the breakdown – labour hours, travel, each part with its name and quantity. A reputable technician won't mind itemising. Third, get the estimate in writing (a WhatsApp message is fine) before work starts, especially if the job might open up walls or floors and reveal extra damage. In older Sanur villas that's common: behind one cracked tile there may be three corroded fittings. Agree on how additional findings will be priced so there are no surprises at the end.

Questions

Do plumbers in Sanur charge by the hour or per job?

Most small jobs – tap swap, drain clearing, pool filter backwash reset – are quoted as a flat rate once the scope is known. Larger or uncertain work like repiping a bathroom or replacing a septic-tank connection is often estimated per hour after diagnosis. Either way, confirm the pricing method before the technician starts.

Can I buy my own parts to save money?

Yes, and many villa owners do this for simple items like washers, hose connectors, or a standard PPR fitting. Just make sure you match the size and thread type exactly. For anything involving pressure-rated pipes, gas lines, or electrical components near water, let the technician supply and fit the part – it keeps the work safe and accountable.

How far in advance should I book?

For planned maintenance – annual rooftop tank cleaning, pool pump service, pre-rainy-season pipe check – a few days' notice is plenty. If multiple areas of your compound need work, batching them into one visit saves on travel time. Emergencies are handled same-day, but the rate will reflect the urgency.
