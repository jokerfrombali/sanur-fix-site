If water is sitting in your kitchen sink or pooling in the shower for more than a minute or two, you most likely have a partial blockage in the trap or the short pipe below it. In most Sanur villas the cause is simple: hair, soap scum, food particles, or mineral build-up from PDAM tap water. The good news is that many of these clogs can be cleared at home with basic tools before you need to call anyone over.

Why Clogs Happen in Bali (and Especially in Older Sanur Villas)

The everyday culprits are the same everywhere: hair wrapping around the shower strainer, grease and rice grains settling in the kitchen P-trap, and soap residue coating the inside of the pipe. What makes Sanur a little different is the water itself. PDAM supply has a moderate mineral content, and if your villa was built in the 1990s or early 2000s the internal plumbing may still be thin-walled PVC whose joints have shifted slightly over the years. That tiny gap becomes a perfect shelf for debris to collect on.

Add the tropical humidity and salt-laden sea air blowing off the beach, and any exposed metal fittings near the drain corrode faster than they would in a temperate climate. During the rainy season (roughly November to March) you also notice more organic matter — leaves, insects, mud — getting into outdoor drains, which can back up into indoor lines if the septic system is struggling.

  • Hair and soap scum – the number-one cause of slow shower drains
  • Food particles and cooking oil – common in kitchen sinks
  • Limescale build-up from PDAM tap water in older pipes
  • Shifted PVC joints in villas built before 2005 creating small traps for debris
  • Corroded metal parts accelerated by coastal humidity

What You Can Do Yourself First

Before reaching for anything chemical, try the simplest mechanical fixes. Start with hot (not boiling) water: run a steady stream at about 70 °C for two or three minutes. Boiling water can soften and warp thin PVC, so keep it below that. Next, mix half a cup of baking soda down the drain, follow with a cup of white vinegar, let it fizz for twenty minutes, then flush with hot water again.

For a stubborn sink clog, place a bucket under the U-shaped P-trap, loosen the slip nuts with an adjustable wrench, and pull the trap out. You will almost always find a plug of hair, food, or scale inside. Rinse it, reassemble, and test. In the shower, lift off the strainer cover first; if there is a removable screen underneath, clean that too.

  • Run hot (≤ 70 °C) water steadily for 2–3 minutes
  • Baking soda + vinegar, wait 20 min, flush with hot water
  • Remove and inspect the P-trap (bucket underneath!)
  • Clean the shower strainer and any secondary mesh screen
  • Check the basin overflow hole for trapped debris

Plunger and Drain Snake: Doing It Right

A standard cup plunger works well on sinks and showers. Fill the basin with just enough water to cover the rubber cup, seal it firmly over the drain, and push-pull briskly eight to ten times. The pressure wave dislodges soft blockages that hot water alone could not move.

If the plunger does not help, a flexible drain snake (a coiled steel cable about one metre long) is your next tool. Feed it into the drain while turning the handle clockwise; when you feel resistance, keep twisting gently and then pull back out. You should see hair or gunk wrapped around the tip. For a shower drain, remove the strainer first so the cable can enter freely. Do not jam the snake hard into thin PVC — you risk puncturing the pipe wall.

Why Aggressive Chemical Drain Openers Are a Bad Idea Here

Those caustic, lye-based liquids sold in every minimarket generate intense heat as they dissolve organic matter. In a modern copper or thick-PPR system that might be tolerable once in a while, but in an older Sanur villa with mixed PVC-and-metal plumbing the heat can soften joints, warp the trap, and accelerate corrosion on any remaining chrome or brass fittings. Over time the pipe walls thin and eventually crack, turning a small clog into a leak under your floor tile.

There is also a safety issue: if you pour one brand of opener and it does not work, then add a different one (especially anything containing bleach or acid), toxic chlorine gas can form. If you ever smell sharp fumes after mixing products, stop immediately, open windows, get to fresh air, and call a professional rather than adding more liquid.

Finally, whatever goes down the drain ends up in your septic tank or the local drainage channel. Repeated chemical dumps upset the bacterial balance in the septic, which is already doing its best in the tropical heat.

When It Is Time to Call a Plumber

Book a visit if two or more fixtures are draining slowly at the same time — that points to a blockage further along the main line rather than in a single trap. Also call if water starts backing up from the shower when you flush the toilet, if you notice a persistent sewage smell near the floor drain, or if the septic area behind the villa is bubbling or overflowing. In Sanur many villas have their own small septic system, and once the tank needs pumping no amount of DIY will fix the slow drain upstairs.

If you have tried hot water, baking soda, a plunger, and a snake with no improvement, it is safer to let someone with proper equipment (a motorised auger, a camera scope) locate the exact point of restriction before any further force is applied to old pipes.

  • Multiple drains slow simultaneously – likely a main-line issue
  • Water backs up between fixtures (shower ↔ toilet)
  • Sewage odour or bubbling near the septic field
  • DIY methods exhausted with no change after 24 hours

Questions

Can I pour boiling water down my sink drain?

Avoid true boiling water, especially on PVC traps common in older Sanur villas. Water at about 70 °C is hot enough to loosen grease without softening plastic pipe walls. If your plumbing is all copper or PPR, slightly hotter water is fine, but when in doubt stay below 80 °C.

My shower drains slowly but the kitchen sink is perfectly fine. Is that serious?

Usually not. A single slow fixture most often means a local blockage — hair in the strainer, scale in the trap, or a small shift in one joint. It rarely indicates a whole-house problem unless you start seeing other drains affected within a day or two.

How do I tell if the slow drain is caused by the septic tank rather than the pipe?

If every drain in the villa slows at once, you hear gurgling from multiple points, and there is a musty smell near the floor drain or the septic area behind the house, the tank is probably full or the outlet line is blocked. In that case no amount of clearing the individual traps will help; the system needs inspection and possibly pumping.
