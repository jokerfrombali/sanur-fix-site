Most Sanur villas run on a small underground septic tank rather than city sewerage. You only notice it when something goes wrong—slow drains, that unmistakable smell creeping into the garden. Here is how often you actually need to pump it, what the job looks like from start to finish, and how pricing works.

Warning Signs Your Septic Tank Is Full

A healthy septic system is silent. The moment you start noticing problems, the tank is probably close to capacity or the drain field is clogged. In older Sanur villas the tank can sit under a concrete patio slab or buried in the back garden behind the pool, so you may not even know exactly where it is until an issue forces you to look.

The most common early signals are slow-draining toilets or showers, gurgling sounds coming from floor drains after you flush, and a sour, sewage-like odour near the ground surface above the tank. If you see standing water pooling in the garden after a light rain—or during the dry season—that usually means the soakaway area is saturated and the liquid has nowhere to go.

  • Toilet takes more than two flushes to clear
  • Foul smell around the tank lid or nearby lawn
  • Garden soil feels spongy or stays wet for days
  • Multiple drains backing up at the same time
  • Unusual bubbling in the toilet bowl when no one is flushing

How Often Should You Pump It?

There is no single answer because every villa is different. A two-bedroom rental with one bathroom and two tenants will fill its tank far slower than a family of five running three bathrooms, a kitchen sink, and a washing machine daily. As a rough guide, a typical 1,000–2,000 litre septic tank in Sanur needs pumping every two to three years under normal use.

A few local factors shift that timeline. During the rainy season (roughly November to March) more grey-water enters the system from showers and laundry, which accelerates sludge build-up. If your villa draws from a well rather than PDAM mains, the water may carry more sediment that settles into the tank. Older properties built before the 2000s often have smaller tanks and clay pipe connections that clog faster than modern PVC setups.

What the Pumping Process Actually Looks Like

You do not need to be home for most of it, but you should let the technician know where the tank lid is. In many Sanur villas the lid is a square concrete or plastic cover set flush with the ground, sometimes hidden under a small patch of grass or a potted plant. If you cannot find it, the technician will locate it with a probe—this takes ten minutes at most.

Once the lid is open, a vacuum truck hose is lowered into the tank. The operator pumps out the liquid layer on top and the thick sludge settled at the bottom. For a standard residential tank this takes anywhere from twenty minutes to an hour depending on how full it is and how accessible the lid is (a tight garden corner with a narrow access road adds time). The waste goes into the truck's sealed hold and is taken to a licensed disposal site outside the village; you are not responsible for where it ends up.

One safety note: if you smell strong hydrogen-sulphide gas when the lid is lifted, step back and let the professional handle it. Do not light anything nearby and keep children and pets away from the opening until the job is done.

How Much Does It Cost?

There is no fixed rate card for septic pumping in Sanur because the price depends on several variables: the tank's volume, how much sludge has accumulated, whether the lid is easily reachable or buried under concrete that needs cutting, and how far the disposal site is from your address. A small 1,000-litre tank in an open driveway will cost less than a 3,000-litre unit tucked behind a pool wall with limited truck access.

The practical way to get a number is simple: take two or three photos of the tank area (the lid, the surrounding ground, any obstacles) and send them over WhatsApp. The technician reviews the images, asks one or two questions about household size and last pump date, and gives you a quote before any truck is dispatched. You approve the figure first, so there are no surprises at the gate.

What to Do After the Tank Is Pumped

While the tank is empty the technician can inspect the interior walls and inlet/outlet pipes for cracks, root intrusion, or broken seals. If something looks off they will point it out while you are still standing there—much cheaper to fix now than after the tank refills and leaks into your garden soil.

Once the job is finished, flush every toilet twice, run all taps for a minute, and do a quick laundry cycle if possible. This re-establishes normal flow through the system. Over the next few days keep an eye on drain speeds; if everything clears normally you are good to go.

Going forward, avoid pouring cooking grease, coffee grounds, or strong chlorine-based cleaners down your drains. Chlorine in particular kills the beneficial bacteria that break down solids inside the tank, which means sludge builds up faster and you end up pumping more often than necessary. A simple log of the last pump date (a sticky note on the fridge works) keeps you from forgetting until the smell reminds you.

Questions

Can I just open the lid and hose the tank out myself?

Please don't. The sludge layer at the bottom contains concentrated waste and hydrogen-sulphide gas. Hosing water into a full tank can also overflow the drain field and contaminate your garden soil. A vacuum truck with proper PPE handles it safely and disposes of the waste legally.

Where does the pumped-out waste actually go?

The truck carries it to a licensed disposal facility outside Sanur village. You do not need to arrange transport or pay an extra fee for this; it is part of the standard pumping service. If a provider asks you to take the waste somewhere yourself, walk away.

I have no idea where my septic tank is buried. Is that a problem?

Not really. The technician brings a locating probe and will find the lid in a few minutes. If the villa was built before 2010 there may be two tanks (one for black-water, one for grey-water), so expect the search to cover a slightly larger area. Just keep the path from the road clear of parked scooters on the day.
