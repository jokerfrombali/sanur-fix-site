Leaves, Insects & Frogs in Your Sanur Pool – What To Do

Living in Sanur means your pool is an open bowl sitting under tropical trees, monsoon rain and a constant parade of insects. Leaves, dragonflies and the occasional frog are not a sign something is wrong with your water; they are simply part of life here. A few small habits and the right tools keep the mess manageable without spending hours every day.

Why There Is So Much Debris in Bali

Sanur sits between the Indian Ocean and a dense belt of coconut palms, frangipani, banyan and mango trees. The sea breeze carries fine salt film and organic dust onto every flat surface, including your pool. During the rainy season (roughly November to March) a single afternoon downpour can drop dozens of leaves into the water overnight.

Older villas along Jl. Danau Poso or the smaller lanes near the beach often have mature trees that were planted decades ago. Their canopies overhang the pool area, so you will always get more debris than someone with a freshly built villa and young landscaping.

  • Expect heavier leaf fall during the dry-season wind months (June–September) when strong gusts shake the canopy.
  • Rainy-season storms wash soil particles and insect carcasses off the roof into the pool within minutes.
  • The warm, humid air means algae grows faster once organic matter settles on the tile, so prompt removal matters more than in cooler climates.

The Net and Skimmer Routine

A simple mesh pool net (the long-handled skimmer you see at any hardware shop in Sanur) is your first line of defence. Sweep the surface twice a day: once in the morning before the heat drives insects out, and once after sunset when dragonflies and moths land on still water.

Check the skimmer basket every time you run the pump. A clogged basket reduces circulation, lets leaves sink to the bottom, and makes the filter work harder. Empty it, rinse it under the tap, and put it back. It takes thirty seconds.

If you notice a thick layer of fine silt on the pool floor after a storm, do not just vacuum it away. That silt often contains decomposing plant material that will keep feeding algae for days. Shock-dose the chlorine as per your product's label, let the pump run overnight, then vacuum to waste.

  • Keep a spare net handle in the garden shed; they snap easily in the tropical sun.
  • Wear rubber gloves when handling the skimmer basket – stagnant water plus organic sludge is not pleasant.
  • Never mix different chlorine products or add acid while the pump is off. If you are unsure about dosing, stop and call a professional.

A Pool Cover or Mesh Tent

A fitted mesh cover (sometimes called a pool tent) is the single most effective way to cut leaf and insect entry by 80–90 percent. In Sanur you can have one made to measure from local fabric shops near the market, or order a custom frame-and-mesh structure. The cost depends entirely on the size and shape of your pool; send photos and dimensions on WhatsApp and a technician can quote you directly.

Mesh covers let rain through but block leaves, frogs and larger insects. A solid vinyl cover blocks everything including rain, which means you must lift it before any downpour or standing water will breed mosquitoes on top.

If a full cover feels like overkill, at minimum drape a large tarp or old sailcloth over the pool during heavy rain. It is not elegant, but it saves you an hour of netting afterwards.

  • Inspect the mesh for tears every month; a small hole lets in exactly the frog you were trying to keep out.
  • Secure the edges with sandbags or clips so the sea breeze does not peel the cover off overnight.
  • Clean the top of the cover weekly – salt film and bird droppings build up fast in Sanur's air.

Frogs and Insects

Tree frogs are harmless and actually eat mosquitoes, but they do not belong in your swimming water. If you hear croaking at night, switch on the pool light and gently scoop the frog out with the net. Place it under a nearby bush or in the garden soil. Do not flush it down the drain or use chemicals to drive it away; a mild chlorine shock is enough to make the water unattractive without harming the animal.

Dragonflies, moths and beetles land on still water to drink or lay eggs. The simplest deterrent is keeping the surface moving: run the pump longer each evening (an extra 30–60 minutes) so the gentle current discourages landing. A small fountain jet pointed across the pool helps too.

If mosquito larvae appear in the skimmer basket or along the tile line, check that the water level has not dropped below the overflow weir. Standing water in the gutter around the pool edge is where larvae breed. Clear the gutter, then treat the pool with a larvicide tablet as directed on the packaging.

  • Do not pour undiluted insecticide into the pool – it will kill beneficial bacteria and can irritate swimmers' eyes.
  • A frog in the pool is not an emergency; one gentle scoop and it is gone for the season.
  • If you see large numbers of dead insects floating, vacuum them out before they decompose and spike your pH.

Trees Around Your Villa

The most effective long-term fix is managing the canopy above and beside your pool. Ask your gardener to prune branches that overhang the pool area by at least two metres. In Bali this is usually done once or twice a year, ideally just before the rainy season when growth is slowest.

Coconut palms drop whole fronds that are heavy and fibrous; if one lands in the pool it can tear a mesh cover. Mango trees shed fruit that ferments underwater within 48 hours and turns the water brown. If you have either species directly over the pool, discuss pruning angles with your neighbour or the villa manager so the fruit falls onto grass, not water.

You do not need to remove the trees. A well-pruned banyan or frangipani still gives shade and beauty while cutting leaf fall dramatically. The key is regular maintenance rather than a single big cut.

  • After any major storm, walk the perimeter and check for broken branches hanging low – they will come down in the next gust.
  • Keep the garden bed around the pool free of mulch piles where beetles and larvae hide during the day.
  • If a neighbouring tree is the main source of debris, a polite conversation (or a shared gardener) solves more problems than any amount of netting.

Questions

How often should I skim leaves out of my Sanur pool?

Twice a day in dry-season wind months and at least once daily after every rain shower. In the rainy season, check after each downpour because a single storm can fill the skimmer basket with wet leaves that sink quickly if left.

Will a mesh pool cover stop frogs from jumping in?

A properly fitted mesh cover with no gaps at the edges will block most adult frogs. Small froglets can squeeze through worn mesh, so inspect the fabric monthly and repair tears promptly. If you have a persistent problem, add a short overhang frame on the side nearest the garden bushes.

Is it safe to use chlorine shock while there are insects or a frog in the pool?

Yes, standard chlorine shock doses are not lethal to a frog that is scooped out within an hour. However, do not mix different chlorine products, and never add acid or algaecide while the pump is off. If you smell strong fumes or feel eye irritation, ventilate the area and call a professional before adding anything else.
