Moving into a Sanur villa often means dealing with flat-pack boxes from the shop plus teak chairs that have sat through three rainy seasons. The salt air and monsoon humidity quietly weaken joints, swell panels, and lift finishes long before you notice anything is wrong. Here is what actually works when something needs assembling, fixing, or saving.

Assembling Flat-Pack Furniture in Your Villa

Most expats arrive with a stack of cardboard boxes and an instruction booklet in four languages. Before you start, lay every part out on the floor and check against the parts list. In my experience the most common issue in Bali deliveries is missing cam-lock bolts or stripped dowel holes because the box was handled roughly on the last stretch of Jalan Raya Sanur.

Work on a hard surface, not on the tile directly if you can avoid it; a folded blanket protects both the piece and your floor. Tighten screws in the order shown in the manual rather than all at once. If a joint feels loose after assembly, a small amount of wood glue on the dowel and a clamp for twenty minutes will hold far better than re-tightening the screw.

  • Photograph every bag of hardware before you open it so you can spot a missing piece quickly.
  • If a screw strips its hole, fill it with a toothpick dipped in PVA glue, let it dry, then re-drill a pilot hole.
  • Leave the packaging flat for two weeks in case you need to return a part to the shop.

Repairing Teak Furniture

Teak is the default material in Balinese villas, and for good reason: its natural oils resist rot. But decades of sea air, direct UV from the western sun, and the wet season take their toll. You will see silver-grey weathering on outdoor sets, hairline cracks along grain lines, and joints that creak when you shift your weight.

For surface scratches or dull patches, sand lightly with 180-grit paper along the grain, wipe off dust with a damp cloth, and apply a thin coat of teak oil or a neutral penetrating oil. Let it soak for twenty minutes, then buff off the excess. Repeat once after a week if the wood still looks thirsty. For a loose joint on a chair leg, chisel out old glue, clean both faces, re-glue with a marine-grade epoxy, and clamp until cured overnight.

If the crack runs deep into the seat rail or a leg has split past the mid-point, do not try to fill it with filler alone. The structural load will reopen it within a month. That is a job where a technician can reinforce it internally.

Wardrobes, Cabinets, and Swollen Doors

Sanur's humidity swings are brutal on particleboard and MDF wardrobes. After a long stretch of rain, doors stick, drawers bind, and hinges pull out of their plates. In older villas near the beach, PDAM pipe leaks behind the kitchen cabinet or a slow drip from the rooftop tank feed line can swell an entire panel in a single wet season.

Start by loosening every hinge screw slightly, closing the door, and tightening them back down so the weight sits evenly. If a drawer runner is corroded, replace the pair rather than oiling one side. For a swollen door that no longer closes, plane off 1–2 mm along the edge or sand with coarse paper; check clearance after each pass.

  • Keep a small bottle of silicone spray for squeaky runners; avoid WD-40 on wood as it dries out the finish.
  • If you smell musty mould inside a wardrobe after the rains, remove all contents, wipe with a vinegar-water solution, and leave the doors open for two days before replacing items.
  • Check under the sink cabinet monthly for slow drips from the PDAM supply line before the swelling becomes permanent.

Termites: The Silent Problem in Old Villas

Bali's warm, humid climate is ideal for subterranean termites, and older Sanur villas built on wooden posts are especially vulnerable. You may not see any damage until a chair leg crumbles when you lean on it, or you notice fine sawdust-like frass beneath a bookshelf.

Tap solid-looking legs and rails with a coin. A hollow sound means the interior has been eaten away even if the surface looks intact. If you find active mud tubes on a wall near your furniture or live insects in a joint, stop using the piece and call a licensed pest-control operator to treat the structure. Do not mix chemical sprays yourself around food areas or children's rooms; get professional advice first.

Once treated, replace any structurally compromised parts rather than gluing them back together. Termite-damaged wood will fail again within a season.

Ongoing Care Through the Seasons

A ten-minute monthly check saves you from expensive repairs later. Wipe down teak surfaces with a dry cloth after heavy rain so salt residue does not etch the finish. In the dry season (May–September), UV accelerates fading, so move outdoor cushions and rotate chairs if they sit in direct afternoon sun along the beachfront road.

Before the rainy season hits around October, inspect all exterior joints, re-apply oil to bare teak, and make sure no water is pooling under a bench or table leg. Indoors, keep wardrobes at least 5 cm off the tile floor so air can circulate underneath and panels do not wick moisture upward.

  • Re-oil outdoor teak every six months during the wet season and once a year in the dry.
  • Lift furniture legs with small rubber feet to stop them dragging on polished tile and picking up grit that scratches the surface.
  • After any plumbing work near a kitchen cabinet, run the tap for two minutes and check the underside for seepage before closing it back up.

Questions

Can I assemble IKEA-style flat-pack furniture myself in my Sanur villa?

Yes, most pieces are straightforward if you follow the manual and use a proper Phillips screwdriver rather than a coin. The main risk here is missing hardware from rough delivery handling. If a joint still feels loose after gluing and clamping, send me a photo on WhatsApp and I will tell you whether it needs a reinforcing bracket or just another clamp cycle.

My teak dining table has deep cracks and grey patches. Is it beyond repair?

Surface weathering and shallow cracks respond well to sanding and re-oiling. Deep structural splits in the apron or leg joints need epoxy reinforcement or a replacement part. Send a clear photo of the worst crack in daylight; I can judge from the image whether it is cosmetic or load-bearing and quote accordingly.

How do I know if termites have damaged my wooden furniture?

Look for fine powdery frass under legs, hollow-sounding wood when tapped with a coin, and small exit holes about 2 mm wide. If you see live insects or mud tubes, stop using the piece immediately and contact a licensed pest operator before treating anything yourself. Once confirmed, structurally affected parts should be replaced, not glued back together.
