Kitchen Grease Trap Guide for Sanur Villas

A grease trap is a small device that catches fat, oil, and food particles before they travel into your drain line. In Sanur villas—especially older ones with narrow PVC piping and on-site septic tanks—it is one of the simplest ways to prevent slow drains and costly blockages. Below you will find why you need one, how it separates grease from water, how to clean it safely, how often to do so, and what a proper installation looks like.

Why Your Kitchen Needs One

Bali home cooking leans heavily on coconut milk, palm oil, deep-fried sambal, and rich curries. Every time you pour that liquid down the sink, a thin film of fat coats the inside of your pipe. Over weeks it thickens, traps rice grains and vegetable bits, and slowly narrows the flow.

Most Sanur villas built in the 1990s or earlier have relatively small-diameter drainage lines that were never designed for this volume of grease. Add PDAM water (or well water) with its natural mineral content, and the residue hardens faster than it would in soft-water regions.

The end result is usually a clog two or three metres downstream—or worse, a backup into your septic tank. Grease floating on top of a septic chamber kills the bacteria that break down solids, and once that balance tips, you are looking at a full pump-out and possible tank repair.

A grease trap sits between your sink and the main line and intercepts the problem before it starts.

How a Grease Trap Works

The principle is simple gravity separation. Hot, greasy water enters the unit and slows down as it spreads across a wider internal surface. Because fat is lighter than water, it rises to the top while the cleaner water sinks and flows out through an outlet baffle toward your drain line.

Solid food particles—rice, herb stems, eggshell fragments—settle to the bottom. The middle layer of relatively clean water exits through a short pipe that is positioned below the grease layer but above the sediment layer.

Most residential traps are stainless-steel or heavy-gauge plastic boxes, roughly 30–50 litres, mounted under the kitchen sink or just outside the wall where the drain exits. They have a hinged lid so you can reach inside for cleaning.

If your villa has a small commercial-style kitchen (common in rental properties with a full-time cook), a larger inline unit may be more appropriate. A technician can size it from photos of your current setup.

Cleaning Your Grease Trap

Start by placing a bucket underneath the unit and removing the lid. You will see three layers: solid waste at the bottom, a thick film of congealed oil on top, and greyish water in between. Scrape the solids into a sealed bag and pour the liquid grease into a heat-resistant container for disposal—never back down the drain.

Rinse the interior with hot water (not boiling, to avoid warping plastic parts) and a stiff brush. Wipe the baffle plate and the outlet pipe. Replace the lid and run cold water through the sink for a minute to flush any remaining residue.

A few safety notes. If you smell strong gas or notice bubbling near the trap while working, stop immediately and ventilate the area; call a plumber before continuing. Do not mix chemical drain cleaners with any residual grease or detergent already in the unit—the reaction can splash hot caustic liquid. And keep electrical appliances (kettle, toaster) well away from the wet work area.

If after cleaning the drain is still sluggish, or you see a persistent oily ring around the floor drain, the blockage has likely moved further down the line. That is when a professional inspection with a camera makes sense.

How Often Should You Clean It?

For a typical family kitchen where two to three people cook daily, clearing the trap every six to eight weeks keeps it functioning well. If your villa has a full-time cook preparing meals for four or more people, monthly cleaning is more realistic.

During the rainy season (roughly November to March) organic matter decomposes faster in septic systems, so even a small amount of extra grease can tip the balance. Many owners find they need to clean slightly more often in those months.

Watch for these signs that it is time: the sink drains noticeably slower than usual, a faint rancid-oil smell lingers under the counter, or you see a thin iridescent film on the water surface inside the trap. Catching it early means a ten-minute scrape rather than an emergency call-out.

Installing a Grease Trap in Your Villa

The ideal location is as close to the sink as possible—ideally within the first 50 cm of the waste pipe—so that grease does not have far to travel before being captured. The unit must sit level; even a slight tilt will let grease slip past the baffle and into the main line.

In older Sanur villas the drain exit may be at an awkward height or angle. A plumber will check the slope, cut the existing pipe if needed, and fit the trap with proper gaskets. If the kitchen floor has no access panel, the installation may involve a short section of exposed pipe along the wall, which is normal and easy to paint over later.

Because every villa's plumbing layout differs, the best way to get a fair quote is to send clear photos of your current sink area, the visible drain pipe, and any existing trap (if one was fitted by a previous owner) to a local technician on WhatsApp. They can advise whether a standard under-sink box is sufficient or whether you need a larger inline unit, and give you a price based on what they see.

Questions

Can I just pour hot water down the drain instead of installing a trap?

Hot water temporarily melts grease, but it simply pushes it further into the pipe where it re-solidifies in a cooler section. Over time this creates a stubborn plug that is much harder to remove than the soft film a trap catches daily. A trap is a far more reliable long-term solution for a villa kitchen.

Will a grease trap slow down my sink drainage?

A correctly sized and level-installed trap adds only a few seconds to the emptying cycle. If your sink feels sluggish after installation, the unit is probably tilted, the outlet baffle is misaligned, or the inlet pipe diameter is too small. A quick adjustment by a plumber usually resolves it.

Do I still need regular septic pumping if I have a grease trap?

Yes. The trap handles fat and large solids from the kitchen, but human waste, toilet paper, and other household liquids still load the septic tank. Regular inspection and pumping remain part of normal villa maintenance in Sanur, especially with well-water systems where mineral deposits build up over time.
