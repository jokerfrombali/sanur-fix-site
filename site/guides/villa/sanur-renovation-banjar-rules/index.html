Renovating in Sanur: Neighbors, Banjar & Noise Rules

Before you hire a contractor to knock through a wall or re-tile the bathroom, check what your banjar expects from you. In Sanur, villas sit close together and community norms around noise and timing are real – not just suggestions. Get these basics right and your project runs smoother for everyone.

Know Your Banjar's Ground Rules

In most of Sanur, every villa falls under a local banjar adat. The banjar head (kelian) keeps informal but firm expectations about construction activity on their patch. There is no single written code you can download; rules vary block by block and sometimes house by house.

If you are renting long-term, ask your landlord whether they have already registered any planned works with the banjar. If you own the property, visit the kelian before the first truck arrives. A short conversation saves a lot of awkward phone calls later.

  • Ask whether the banjar requires a written notice for work longer than a few days.
  • Confirm if there are restrictions on concrete mixing, demolition, or heavy machinery at ground level.
  • Check whether PDAM water shut-offs for plumbing work need a heads-up to the whole lane.

Noise: What Neighbors Actually Tolerate

Sanur lanes are narrow. A jackhammer in one villa echoes down the whole row within seconds. Most neighbors will not complain after ten minutes of drilling – but a full day of it, three days running, changes the mood fast.

As a practical rule, keep the loudest tasks (demolition, concrete cutting, large-scale tiling) clustered into as few days as possible. Spread lighter work – painting, electrical wiring, pool resurfacing – across quieter stretches so the overall volume stays manageable.

Sea air also matters here: salt-laden humidity accelerates corrosion on metal tools and fittings. If your contractor is working near the pool or on rooftop tanks, remind them that rusted brackets or corroded pipe clips will need replacing sooner than they would inland.

Timing Your Work Around the Season

Rainy season (roughly November to March) turns any open excavation into a mud pit within an hour. If your plan involves opening the ground for septic-tank inspection, re-piping PDAM connections, or laying new drainage, schedule those jobs in the dry months whenever you can.

Conversely, the hot, dry stretch from May to September is ideal for exterior plastering, roof repairs, and pool tile replacement because surfaces cure properly. Just be mindful that midday heat makes long outdoor shifts uncomfortable for workers, so expect slower progress between 11 am and 3 pm.

  • Avoid breaking up floors or walls during peak rain; water ingress into old villa foundations is a real risk.
  • Plan pool chemical work and chlorine handling away from windy afternoons when fumes drift toward neighbors' patios.
  • If you smell gas while cutting through a wall or floor, stop immediately and call a professional before continuing.

Notifying Neighbors Before You Start

You do not need a lawyer's letter. A simple note slipped under the two nearest doors – or a friendly knock with a smile – saying what you are doing, roughly how long it will take, and who to text if something gets too loud goes a long way.

In Sanur many neighbors are other expats or local families who have lived on the lane for years. They remember which contractor was polite last time and which one wasn't. A five-minute introduction sets the tone for the whole project.

If your work involves a temporary water shut-off (common when replacing PDAM inlet valves or fixing leaks in rooftop tanks), give at least a day's notice down the lane so people can fill their storage tanks.

When Things Go Sideways

Disputes usually start small: a worker parking on the shared lane, a bag of cement left overnight, a drill running past the agreed quiet window. Address it the same day, calmly, face-to-face. Escalating through the banjar head is fine but should be a second step, not the first.

If a neighbor raises a concern about structural damage, leaking water into their wall, or a septic issue caused by nearby excavation, stop work in that area until a professional has inspected it. Do not mix chlorine-based pool chemicals with any acid products while troubleshooting – call a technician instead.

Keep photos and short video clips of every stage. If a disagreement later turns into a question of what was already damaged versus what happened during your renovation, a dated photo log is the simplest evidence you will have.

Questions

Do I need official permission from the banjar to renovate my Sanur villa?

There is no universal permit system, but most kelians expect at least a verbal heads-up for anything beyond minor cosmetic work. Ask directly; it takes ten minutes and prevents friction.

What hours can contractors reasonably work next door?

Most Sanur neighbors are comfortable with 8 am to 5 pm on weekdays. Early-morning starts before 7 am or weekend demolition tend to draw complaints. Agree a window with your contractor and stick to it.

My neighbor says my plumbing work cracked their floor. What do I do?

Stop the work, document both properties with photos, and invite a licensed plumber or structural inspector to assess the damage before anyone assigns blame. A neutral professional report keeps the conversation practical rather than personal.
