Sanur's coastal air keeps indoor humidity very high for most of the year, and solid-wood doors absorb that moisture quickly. The result is a panel that swells by a few millimetres and no longer meets the frame cleanly. In most cases you can bring the door back without replacing it. Work through the fixes below in order, starting with the simplest.

Why Humidity Swells Your Door

The sea breeze off Sanur Bay carries salt-laden moisture into every open window and gap around your door. During the rainy months (roughly November to April) indoor air stays damp for weeks on end. Solid teak, mahogany, or jati panels expand as they take up water; even a two-millimetre swell is enough to make the bottom edge drag on the threshold or the side rub the frame.

Before you touch any wood, rule out an active leak. Check the PDAM supply pipe where it enters the wall behind the door, look under the rooftop tank for overflow staining, and inspect the ceiling above for damp patches from a leaking gutter. If you find standing water or a slow drip, fix that first — sanding a wet door will only give you a temporary result.

  • Run a dehumidifier near the doorway for a week during peak humidity to see if the panel shrinks back on its own.
  • Wipe the door surface with a dry cloth after heavy rain if it sits close to an open window.
  • If the villa uses well water, check that the pressure valve isn't weeping onto the lower rail of the door.

Adjust the Hinge Side Before You Cut Anything

A swollen door often just needs a millimetre more clearance at the hinge side. Loosen the two screws in each hinge leaf by about a quarter turn — don't remove them entirely. Close the door slowly and feel where it catches. Nudge the hinge pin so the panel shifts one or two millimetres away from the rubbing edge, then retighten the screws.

If the problem is at the latch side, move the strike plate. Unscrew it, shift it down or sideways by the amount the bolt is missing, mark the new screw positions, and re-drill. A thin shim behind the plate can also take up play in older frames common in pre-2000 Sanur villas.

Trim the Panel Where It Rubs

Once the door is as adjusted as you can get it, check the remaining gap with a credit card or a thin piece of cardboard. If the bottom edge still drags on the threshold, lay the door flat on two sawhorses and sand the underside with 80-grit paper, working in long strokes along the grain. Remove no more than one millimetre per pass; re-check the fit after every few passes.

For a thicker swell, a hand plane set to a shallow cut will be faster than sanding. Keep the blade parallel to the face of the door and let the tool do the work — forcing it will leave a step that catches your foot. After trimming, run a fine 120-grit pass over the whole area so the finish blends in.

  • Always trim from the hinge side first if both edges rub; the latch side needs its full width for the bolt to engage.
  • Wear a dust mask — Bali wood dust mixed with humidity makes a fine, sticky powder that's rough on the lungs.
  • If the door is painted or varnished, touch up the sanded strip with matching paint before the next rainy week sets in.

Replace Worn Hinges Before They Warp the Frame

Salt air corrodes steel hinges within a couple of seasons, especially on doors near the beachfront road. You'll notice the screws spinning in stripped holes, the pin rattling loosely, or the leaf bending slightly out of plane. A bent hinge pulls the panel off-centre and makes even a well-trimmed door bind at one corner.

Swap them for stainless-steel butt hinges rated for exterior use. Drill new pilot holes if the old ones are stripped, fill the gaps with wood glue and toothpicks, let it cure overnight, then re-drill. If the frame itself has split around the screw hole, back it out with a dowel and epoxy before driving the new screw home.

Realign or Replace the Lock Mechanism

When the panel swells, the mortise lock bolt often no longer lines up with the strike plate slot. The latch may catch halfway, or the deadbolt grinds against the metal plate. Loosen the two long screws that hold the lock body to the edge of the door and shift the whole mechanism by a millimetre or two until the bolt drops cleanly into the slot.

If the lock is an older cast-iron type common in pre-2005 villas, the internal springs may have corroded. In that case the cylinder turns but the bolt doesn't throw fully. That's a job for a locksmith or a hardware shop in Sanur rather than a DIY fix — forcing a seized mechanism can snap the cam and leave you locked out of your own villa.

Questions

How long does a swollen wooden door take to shrink back on its own?

In Sanur's dry season (May to October) a well-ventilated room can bring a few millimetres of swell down over three to six weeks. If humidity stays high because of poor airflow or a nearby leak, the door may never fully recover without trimming.

Can I use an electric sander instead of hand sanding?

You can, but go very lightly. A random-orbit sander at low speed with 80-grit paper removes material faster, but it's easy to take off too much in one pass on a thin Bali villa door. Hand-sanding gives you more control and lets you check the fit every thirty seconds.

Should I re-seal my wooden doors every year?

A fresh coat of exterior-grade varnish or penetrating oil once a year, ideally before November when the rains start, keeps moisture out of the grain. It won't stop all swelling, but it slows the cycle noticeably and protects the finish from UV fading near open windows.
