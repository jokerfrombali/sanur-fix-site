The best window for major pool work in Sanur is the dry season, roughly May through September, when you can drain water without a monsoon immediately refilling the basin with silt and leaves. Smaller jobs like tile replacement or pump servicing are fine any month. The real rule is matching the size of the job to your weather forecast so you never finish a repair only to watch three days of rain undo the chemical balance overnight.

Seasons: What the Weather Actually Does to Your Pool

Sanur sits on the east coast, so the sea breeze carries salt particles that settle on metal ladders, skimmer baskets, and pump housings year-round. In the wet months (November to March) that corrosion accelerates because humidity stays above eighty percent for weeks at a stretch. If your stainless-steel fittings are already pitting, waiting until the next dry spell means more rust under the paint by the time you get around to it.

Rain also dilutes chlorine and introduces organic matter—leaves from the frangipani trees, bird droppings, mud from the garden path. A pool that was balanced at 1 ppm free chlorine before a storm can drop below detectable within two days, giving algae a head start in warm water.

  • Dry season (May–Sep): ideal for draining, resurfacing, tile work, and any job where the basin is open.
  • Wet season (Nov–Mar): limit repairs to sealed components—pumps, filters, plumbing under the deck—where rain cannot interfere.
  • Transition months (Apr, Oct): watch the forecast daily; a single afternoon downpour can flood an unfinished repair area.

Timing Your Repair Window

A simple skimmer or light swap takes a couple of hours. Replacing a cracked coping stone or resealing a leaky pipe might need a full day plus curing time. Full resurfacing or structural crack injection runs several days and requires the pool to stay drained, which is why you want a solid dry-season stretch with no forecast rain for at least five working days after the last pour.

Check the Bali weather app the evening before your scheduled visit. If heavy rain is predicted within 48 hours, ask whether the technician can shift the date by one or two days. It costs nothing and saves you from chasing mud out of fresh grout lines.

Preparing Before the Technician Arrives

Clear the pool deck of loungers, umbrellas, and potted plants so there is safe access around the basin. If the job involves the pump room, make sure the electrical panel is reachable and that nobody has stored wet towels or cleaning chemicals on top of the breaker box. Working near water and live electrics is not something to improvise; if you smell gas or see frayed wiring in the pump area, stop and call a licensed electrician first.

Take clear photos of the problem—cracked tile, weeping joint, discoloured plaster—and send them over WhatsApp before the visit. That way the technician brings the right sealant, tile size, or replacement part and does not waste an hour hunting through a hardware shop in Sanur while you wait.

  • Drain the pool partially (to just below the skimmer) for surface work; full drain only if instructed.
  • Switch off the pool pump and isolate the circuit at the main board before anyone opens the equipment housing.
  • Have a bucket, squeegee, and old towel handy for minor clean-up after the repair.
  • If chlorine tablets are nearby, keep them away from any acid-based cleaner—mixing the two releases toxic gas. Stop what you are doing and ventilate immediately.

Managing Guests and Staff During Repairs

In a rental villa or a family compound with long-term tenants, a non-working pool is the number-one complaint. Tell your housekeeper or property manager as soon as you book the repair so they can set guest expectations: "The pool will be out of service Thursday afternoon to Friday morning." A short note on the fridge or a message in the group chat prevents awkward questions at breakfast.

If guests are already staying, offer alternatives—a quick walk along the Sanur beach path, a day trip to Nusa Penida, or simply a cooler shower. Most visitors understand that a properly repaired pool beats a half-fixed one that leaks onto the garden floor three days later.

Planning a Year-Round Pool Schedule

Treat your pool like the septic tank behind the villa: it needs regular attention before something fails. Twice a year (ideally March and September) do a visual inspection—check the waterline for hairline cracks, test the skimmer seal, listen for unusual pump noise, and wipe salt residue off metal fittings. Note anything that looks "off" even if it still works.

Budgeting is straightforward once you know what you have. The cost depends entirely on the scope: a gasket swap is a different job from re-plastering 40 square metres of basin wall. Send photos over WhatsApp and the technician will quote from those images, so you can compare options without committing until you see the number.

Keep a simple log—date, what was done, chemical readings after the work. When you eventually sell or hand over the villa, that paper trail reassures the next owner that the pool has been looked after rather than ignored through two monsoons.

Questions

Can I repair my pool during the rainy season in Bali?

Yes, but only for sealed jobs: replacing a pump impeller, swapping a filter cartridge, fixing an underground pipe joint. Anything that leaves the basin open to the sky—resurfacing, tile replacement, crack injection—should wait for a dry stretch. A single heavy afternoon rain can wash silt into fresh cement and ruin days of work.

How far in advance should I book a pool repair in Sanur?

For small fixes, a day or two is usually enough. For larger jobs like resurfacing or structural repair, give at least one week so the technician can source materials and confirm a clear-weather window. During peak dry-season months (June–August) demand is higher, so booking earlier in the month helps.

Do I need to drain the whole pool before a repair?

Not always. Surface-level work on the upper wall may only need the water lowered below the skimmer. Full draining is reserved for floor resurfacing, major plumbing access, or when the technician specifically asks for it. They will tell you what level they need when they quote from your photos.
