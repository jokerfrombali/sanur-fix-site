You do not need to fly back every fortnight to keep a Sanur villa running well. A few clear routines, one trusted local contact, and a simple photo-report system will cover most of what goes wrong with pools, plumbing, and rooftop tanks between your visits. Here is how expat owners around Sanur handle it day to day.

Who Should Be On Site

The single biggest mistake remote owners make is assuming 'nothing happened' means nothing went wrong. In Sanur many villas are older builds with PDAM mains plus a backup well, a rooftop storage tank, and sometimes a septic system that has never been pumped since construction. All of that needs a human eye at least once a week.

That person does not have to be an engineer. A reliable caretaker, a neighbour you trust, or a local technician on retainer can handle the basics: checking the main water valve, looking under kitchen and bathroom sinks, glancing at the pool surface for algae or oil film, and confirming the rooftop tank is not overflowing after a storm. What matters is consistency, not expertise.

  • Agree in writing who is responsible for daily/weekly checks before you leave.
  • Make sure they know where the main water shut-off and the electrical breaker panel are.
  • If you use a private well, ask them to note any change in flow or taste each visit.

Getting Regular Reports That Are Actually Useful

'Everything is fine' tells you nothing. Ask your on-site contact to send a short structured update every Monday (or twice a week during the November-to-April rainy season when humidity and leaf fall accelerate problems).

A good weekly report covers four things: water pressure and whether the tank refilled normally, pool chlorine and pH readings if they test it, any visible leaks or damp patches, and whether the septic area smells unusual. A 30-second voice note with those points is enough; you do not need a spreadsheet.

  • Dry season (May–October): one check per week is usually sufficient.
  • Rainy season (November–April): two checks per week, especially after heavy downpours that stress gutters and rooftop tanks.
  • Keep a simple shared document so reports stack up and trends become visible over months.

Photo Updates That Save You Money

Words are easy to misread across time zones and language barriers. Photos are not. Ask for five standard shots each visit: the PDAM meter or well gauge, the rooftop tank level indicator, the pool surface from above, the area under the kitchen sink, and the main gutter outlet after rain.

These images let you spot slow drips, rising algae, a corroded fitting, or a clogged drain long before they become an expensive repair. When you eventually call a plumber or pool technician in Sanur, sending those same photos on WhatsApp means the quote is based on what is actually there, not on a vague description.

Keeping a Realistic Budget From Afar

The cost of maintaining an unattended villa in Sanur depends almost entirely on how early small issues are caught. A dripping tap washer, a partially blocked floor drain, or a pool skimmer full of leaves takes minutes to fix. Left for three months in tropical humidity, that same drip can rot a cabinet, the drain can back up into your bathroom, and the pool needs a full chemical reset.

For anything beyond a quick fix—replacing a pump, clearing a septic line, resealing a roof joint—the price varies with the scope of work. The practical approach: send clear photos of the problem on WhatsApp, get a written quote from the technician, and approve only what makes sense. You do not need to guess a number before seeing the job.

  • Set aside a small monthly buffer for consumables (pool chemicals, filter cartridges, a spare tap washer).
  • Budget extra during rainy season for gutter cleaning and algae control.
  • Avoid 'just-in-case' major replacements; let a technician confirm whether a part truly needs swapping.

Handling Emergencies From Another Time Zone

A few situations should never be handled by a caretaker alone, no matter how experienced they are. If there is a gas smell anywhere near the kitchen or water heater, everyone leaves the villa immediately and calls a licensed professional. Do not switch lights on or off, do not light a match to check the stove.

If you suspect an electrical fault near water—a tripped breaker that will not reset, a wet socket in the pool area—cut power at the main breaker and call a qualified electrician. Never mix chlorine with any other chemical to 'boost' the pool; the resulting gas can injure whoever is in the room. Stop, ventilate, and call your pool technician.

For a burst pipe or a tank overflow flooding the ground floor, the first step is always the main shut-off valve. Everything else waits until the water stops moving. Keep a laminated card with the key phone numbers (caretaker, plumber, pool tech, electrician) taped inside the front door so anyone walking in knows where to look.

  • Gas smell: evacuate, call a professional, do not operate switches.
  • Electrics + water: cut the breaker, stay away from the area, call an electrician.
  • Chlorine mixing: stop, open windows, leave the space, call your pool technician.

Questions

How often should someone physically check an empty villa in Sanur?

Once a week during the dry season and twice a week through the rainy months (roughly November to April). The extra visits matter because heavy rain stresses gutters, rooftop tanks, and septic lines, while the humidity accelerates algae growth in pools and mould on walls.

What is the most common problem owners discover after being away for a few weeks?

Slow leaks that were invisible at first—a weeping joint under a sink, a hairline crack in a tile grout line, or a pool pump seal that drips only when warm. In Sanur's heat those small losses compound fast, so a weekly visual check catches them before they damage cabinetry or flooring.

Can I rely entirely on my caretaker without a separate technician on speed dial?

A good caretaker handles day-to-day monitoring and minor tasks, but plumbing, electrical work near water, gas appliances, and pool chemical dosing are best left to a licensed professional. Having one trusted local number you can send photos to keeps things simple and avoids well-meaning DIY fixes that make the original problem worse.
