Every Sanur villa has at least one point where you need to reach into the drainage line or septic tank without digging up the garden. That access point is an inspection hatch or manhole. If yours is missing, rusted shut, or buried under years of tile and paint, small blockages turn into expensive emergencies—especially once the November-to-March rains start.

Why Your Villa Needs Proper Access Points

Sanur villas are not connected to a municipal sewer. Wastewater goes into a two- or three-chamber septic tank and then percolates into a soakaway bed. Because PDAM tap water and well-water taps run constantly—showers, laundry, kitchen—the tanks fill faster than many owners expect. An inspection hatch lets you check sludge levels, clear a partial blockage, or pump the tank before it backs up into your bathroom.

The salt-laden sea air that sits over Sanur also works on metal fittings year-round. A lid that was fine in 2019 can be fused by corrosion by 2025 if it is never opened. Having a properly sized, accessible hatch means a technician can do routine work with a wrench instead of breaking through concrete and re-tilling the floor.

Where Hatches and Manholes Should Be

In a typical Sanur villa layout you will want an access point above each chamber of the septic tank (usually two or three), at every major junction where a branch drain meets the main line, and near the outflow pipe heading toward the leach field. The opening should sit at ground level or just above it so rain does not pool inside.

Older villas built in the 1980s and 90s sometimes have only one small square hole—or none at all—and the rest of the line is sealed under a concrete slab. That makes any future repair far more invasive. If you are renovating a terrace or garden bed, this is the moment to add proper manhole rings while the ground is already open.

Maintaining Them Through the Wet Season

Start your checks in October, before the first heavy storms. Lift each lid fully, brush away leaves and sand that collect around the rim, and look down into the chamber. You should see water well below the top edge; if sludge is within about 30 cm of the surface, the tank needs pumping.

Because the air here carries salt, metal hinges and bolts seize quickly. A thin coat of petroleum jelly on the hinge pin once a month keeps lids operable. Make sure nothing—potted plants, outdoor furniture, a new tile border—is sitting on or blocking the cover. During the monsoon, walk past each hatch after a big storm and confirm no water is standing over the opening.

  • Lift and inspect every lid before November rains begin
  • Clear debris from the rim and inside the opening
  • Apply a light grease to hinges and bolt threads monthly
  • Confirm the cover sits flat with no gap for odour or insects
  • After a major storm, check for pooling or overflow around the ring

Common Problems We See in Sanur Villas

The most frequent call-out is a rusted lid that simply will not lift. The second is a missing cover: someone removed it during a renovation and never put it back, leaving an open hole that is a trip hazard and lets rainwater flood the line. Cracked concrete around the opening is also common in older villas where the slab has shifted with the sandy soil.

If you ever smell hydrogen sulphide (rotten-egg gas) rising from a manhole, stop working, ventilate the area by opening windows, and call a professional. Do not use any electrical tool near the opening until the source of the gas is identified.

  • Rusted or fused lids that cannot be opened
  • Missing covers creating safety hazards for children and pets
  • Cracked or sunken concrete rings letting surface water in
  • Persistent odour indicating a broken seal or a full tank
  • Standing water inside the chamber after heavy rain

Installing or Replacing a Hatch

Adding a new access point is straightforward when the ground is already excavated. A pre-cast concrete ring is set into the trench at the correct depth, levelled, and topped with a metal or composite lid sized to the pipe diameter. For an existing villa where no hatch exists, we cut a clean square through the slab, drop in the ring, and finish the edges so they sit flush with your tile or paving.

The exact scope—whether it is one lid swap, a new ring installation, or clearing a blocked line back to the tank—depends on what we find once we are on site. Send photos of the current setup on WhatsApp and we will quote the job before any work starts.

Questions

How often should I open my septic-tank inspection hatch?

At minimum twice a year: once before the wet season (October) and once mid-dry-season (May). If you run a large household or have a pool that uses well water for top-ups, check quarterly. You are looking at sludge level, debris build-up, and whether the lid still lifts freely.

Can I just cover the opening with a garden paver or a piece of plywood instead of a proper manhole lid?

A flat stone or board works temporarily but will not seal against odour, insects, or rainwater, and it gives you no leverage to lift it when you actually need access. A proper hinged or bolted lid keeps the chamber sealed while still letting you pop it open in seconds. In Sanur's salt air, choose a stainless-steel or heavy-duty composite lid rather than thin galvanised steel.

My outdoor drain smells bad after every shower. Do I need a new hatch?

Not necessarily. The smell usually means the trap is dry, there is a partial blockage upstream, or the vent pipe on the roof is restricted. An inspection hatch helps us diagnose which one it is without breaking up your terrace.
