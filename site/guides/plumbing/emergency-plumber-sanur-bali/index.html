What Counts as a Real Emergency

Not every drip needs a 3 am phone call. A genuine plumbing emergency in a Sanur villa usually falls into one of these categories:

If any of the above is happening, act now. If it is just a slow leak under the kitchen sink or a pool skimmer that is dripping onto the tile, read on before you panic.

  • Active flooding: water pouring from a pipe joint, a split hose, or an overflow that is spreading across the floor faster than you can mop it up.
  • Sewage backup: toilet water rising with a foul smell, or grey water appearing in a shower drain. Stop using all fixtures immediately.
  • Gas smell near a water heater or gas line. Leave the area, do not switch lights on or off, and call a licensed technician right away.
  • Electrical sparks or a wet socket near a running tap. Cut power at the breaker first, then deal with the water. Never touch live wiring while standing in water.

How to Stop the Water Fast

Most Sanur villas get their supply through PDAM (the municipal system) into a rooftop tank, which then feeds gravity-fed taps downstairs. Older properties may also run a well pump. Knowing where each shut-off lives saves precious minutes.

Start at the source. If the flood is coming from the main feed, close the PDAM isolation valve — usually a quarter-turn ball valve on the pipe entering your property, often behind a small gate or in a utility box near the street-side wall. If the problem is one bathroom or kitchen, look for the smaller branch valve under the sink or behind the toilet cistern.

For rooftop-tank systems, there is typically a float-valve shutoff at the top of the tank. A quick climb up (or asking your staff) and turning that valve stops the fill. For well-pump setups, flip the pump's electrical switch off; the pressure in the lines will drop within seconds.

  • Main PDAM valve: usually outside, near the meter or first elbow after the street connection.
  • Rooftop tank fill valve: at the base of the tank, accessible via the internal stair or external ladder.
  • Well pump: an isolated breaker or inline switch, often in a small shed or garage.
  • Individual fixture valves: under sinks, behind toilets, at shower risers.

Who to Call at Night or on a Weekend

If you have a trusted local plumber whose number is already saved in your phone, call them first. In Sanur, a few independent technicians keep a mobile line open for genuine bursts and sewage issues, even on weekends. Send a short video or photo of the leak so they can gauge severity before heading over.

Do not mix any chemicals into a backed-up drain while waiting — chlorine-based products combined with sewer gas create toxic fumes. Do not attempt to cut into a pressurised pipe yourself; the burst can get worse in seconds. If you smell gas or see sparks near water, stop everything and call a professional immediately.

The cost of an emergency visit depends entirely on what needs fixing — a simple valve swap versus chasing a split pipe inside a wall are very different jobs. A fair technician will look at your photos on WhatsApp and give you a rough range before arriving, so there are no surprises.

What Can Wait Until Morning

Here is the honest list: if water is contained, the structure is dry, and nobody is slipping, it is almost certainly fine to wait a few hours.

Rainy-season humidity means small leaks can look dramatic but are actually just condensation on cold pipes. Give it until daylight to confirm whether it is truly leaking or just sweating.

  • A slow drip from a tap washer (put a bowl under it).
  • A pool skimmer or return jet that is weeping onto the deck.
  • A toilet that refills slowly after flushing but does not run continuously.
  • A soft spot or discolouration on a bathroom ceiling with no active dripping.
  • A septic-tank smell near the garden that appears only after heavy rain (likely surface runoff, not a tank failure).

Prepare Before It Happens

Five minutes of preparation in dry weather will save you an hour of fumbling in the dark when something actually breaks.

Walk through your villa once with a torch. Locate every valve, label them with a marker or a sticky note, and take a photo of each one for your phone. Check that the PDAM isolation valve still turns freely — salt-laden sea air around Sanur corrodes brass fittings faster than inland, so a seized valve at 3 am is a real risk on older properties.

Keep a few old towels, a bucket, and a roll of plumber's tape in the utility room. If you have a well pump, make sure the breaker is labelled so anyone in the household can find it without guessing.

  • Label and photograph all shut-off valves now, while things are calm.
  • Test the main PDAM valve once a year; re-grease or replace if it sticks.
  • Save two local technician numbers in your contacts, including a backup.
  • Store absorbent towels and a bucket near the kitchen and bathrooms.
  • If your villa has a septic system, note the last pumping date and keep the access lid clear of plants.

Questions

Can I flush the toilet again after a sewage backup?

No. Every additional flush pushes more waste through the blocked line and increases the chance of overflow into other drains. Stop using all water fixtures until a technician clears the blockage.

My pool water turned green overnight — is that an emergency?

Not usually. Algae blooms happen fast in Bali's warm, humid climate, especially after heavy rain stirs up nutrients. It is a maintenance issue, not a safety one. Avoid mixing chlorine with any other chemical to 'fix' it quickly; send a photo to your pool tech for the right dose.

How do I know if my leak is from PDAM mains or my own plumbing?

Close every tap and shut off the well pump. If the PDAM meter (or the flow indicator on your rooftop tank fill valve) keeps turning, the problem is likely upstream of your internal pipes. If nothing moves but water still appears, the split is inside your villa's plumbing.
