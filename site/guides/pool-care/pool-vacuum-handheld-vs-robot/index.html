Pool Vacuum: Handheld Wand vs Robot – Which One for Your Villa?

Most Sanur villas come with a basic handheld wand, but many owners upgrade to a robot once they've had the pool long enough. Both work; neither is universally better. The right choice depends on your pool size, how often you're home, and whether you want to do it yourself or set-and-forget.

The Handheld Pool Wand

A handheld wand (sometimes called a floor brush or suction head) clips onto the end of a telescopic pole and connects to your skimmer line or a dedicated suction port. You push it along the bottom by hand. It's the cheapest option, easy to store under the pergola, and works on any pool regardless of shape.

In Sanur's sea air, metal parts corrode faster than you'd expect. Rinse the wand after every use and check rubber seals monthly – salt crystals get into joints and make them stiff. If your villa uses well water instead of PDAM, watch for limescale building up inside the hose.

  • Cheapest entry point – no electronics to fail
  • Works in odd-shaped pools where robots struggle
  • You do all the physical work
  • Metal components need regular anti-corrosion care

The Pool Robot

A robot is a self-contained unit that sits on the pool floor, runs on its own pump and filter bag, and navigates the bottom (and often the walls) automatically. You plug it into a GFCI-protected outlet, press start, and walk away. For a typical 8-to-15-metre Sanur villa pool, a run cycle takes one to two hours.

The main advantage is time. If you're working from home or travelling through the rainy season when leaves and dust pile up fast, a robot keeps the bottom clean without you bending over. The downside: electronics in 90% humidity are not happy. Store it indoors or under cover between sessions, and dry the charging contacts before plugging in.

  • Set-and-forget; ideal if you're often out of the house
  • Handles leaves, sand, and fine silt well
  • Needs a dedicated GFCI outlet near the pool edge
  • Electronics need protection from Bali's humidity and salt air

How to Choose Between Them

If your pool is small (under about seven metres), rectangular, and you're home most days, a good wand plus a leaf net will serve you fine for years. If the pool is larger, free-form, or you simply don't want to vacuum twice a week, a robot pays for itself in saved effort.

One practical note for older Sanur villas: check that your skimmer line diameter matches the wand hose, and make sure there's a properly earthed, RCD-protected socket within reach of the pool deck for a robot. If the wiring looks like it dates back twenty years, get an electrician to sort it before you plug anything in – water and old electrics are a combination best avoided.

  • Small, regular pool + you're home daily → wand is enough
  • Large or free-form pool, or you travel often → robot saves time
  • Confirm your electrical setup can safely power a robot
  • Budget depends on the job; send photos on WhatsApp for a quote

How to Actually Vacuum the Pool

Wand method: attach the head to the pole, connect the hose to the skimmer suction port or dedicated return, turn the pump to waste if your system allows, then push the head slowly along the bottom in overlapping passes. Work from the deep end toward the shallow end so debris doesn't settle behind you. Rinse the filter bag when it's half full.

Robot method: lower the unit into the water, let it find its footing, press start. Most models do the floor first, then climb the walls. Don't pull it out mid-cycle unless something looks wrong. When it finishes, empty the filter bag, rinse the impeller, and dry the contacts before storing.

  • Vacuum after rain or heavy leaf fall – don't wait for a thick layer
  • Run the pump at least 30 minutes before vacuuming to circulate loose debris
  • Never mix chlorine tablets with other chemicals while cleaning
  • If you smell gas near the equipment shed, stop and call a professional immediately

Caring for Your Cleaner

Both types last longer if you treat them like outdoor gear in a tropical climate. After each use, rinse off salt residue, sand, and algae film. Store the wand vertically under cover; store the robot on its side (not face-down) in a dry spot away from direct sun.

Once a month, check rubber seals, hoses, and the robot's brushes for cracks or hardening. In the rainy season (roughly November to March), humidity accelerates corrosion on any metal part exposed to sea air. A thin coat of silicone spray on moving joints keeps things smooth. If the robot's motor starts sounding gritty or the wand hose feels brittle, replace the part rather than pushing it – a small fix now beats a flooded filter later.

  • Rinse and air-dry after every session
  • Monthly: inspect seals, brushes, impeller for wear
  • Keep electronics out of direct rain and salt spray
  • Replace worn parts early; don't run a failing pump

Questions

Can I use a handheld wand with my villa's existing skimmer?

Usually yes. The wand hose connects to the skimmer suction port or a dedicated return line. Just make sure the diameter matches your plumbing – most Sanur villas built in the 90s use 50 mm lines, while newer builds may be 75 mm. If you're unsure, send a photo of your skimmer opening on WhatsApp and we'll confirm compatibility.

How often should I vacuum a pool in Sanur?

In the dry season, once a week is usually enough if you keep up with surface skimming. During the rainy months, dust, pollen, and fallen leaves mean you'll want to go twice a week or more. A robot set on a daily short cycle handles this without extra effort on your part.

Is it safe to leave a pool robot plugged in overnight?

Only if the outlet is GFCI/RCD-protected, the cable isn't kinked or sun-cracked, and the unit sits fully submerged or on its designated dock. In Bali's humidity, a damp connector can corrode quietly. If you notice any burning smell, discoloration on the plug, or tripping breakers, unplug it immediately and have an electrician check the circuit before using it again.
