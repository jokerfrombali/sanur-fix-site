Sewage Smell in Your Bathroom? Here's Where It Comes From

That rotten-egg smell creeping up from the floor drain or sink is almost never 'just the weather.' In most Sanur villas it points to one of four things: a dried-out P-trap, a floor drain with no water seal, a blocked roof vent, or a septic tank that needs pumping. The good news: once you know which one it is, the fix is usually straightforward.

The Dried-Out Siphon (P-Trap)

Every sink, shower, and bidet has a U-shaped pipe underneath called a trap. Its job is simple: hold a small pool of water so sewer gas can't travel back into the room. But Sanur's dry season runs hot for months, and if a guest bathroom sits unused for even five or six days, that little pool evaporates. You open the door and hit a wall of stale, sulphurous air.

The quickest test is this: run cold water into the sink or shower for thirty seconds, then sniff again. If the smell drops noticeably within a minute, your trap was simply empty. Pour another cup of water into any overflow hole on the side of the basin while you're at it — those secondary openings also lose their seal.

  • Run water in every bathroom fixture at least twice a week during the dry season.
  • Check under the vanity cabinet: lift the P-trap and look for a visible water pocket. If it's bone-dry, top it up before leaving.
  • If the trap keeps drying out despite regular use, the pipe may be mis-routed or too long; a shortening or re-angle fixes it permanently.

Floor Drain Without a Proper Water Seal

Many older Sanur villas were built with a simple tile-set floor drain — basically a square opening over a shallow channel. There is no real trap inside; the 'seal' is just whatever puddle happens to sit in the channel that day. In the heat, that puddle vanishes overnight, and sewer gas rises straight through the grating into your bathroom.

Newer builds usually have a bottle-trap or T-trap floor drain that holds water properly. But even these can fail if debris (hair, soap scum, sand from the beach) clogs the internal chamber so water drains away instead of pooling.

To check: pour about 500 ml of water into the floor drain and watch. If it disappears in seconds rather than sitting there, the seal isn't working. You either need to clear the blockage or replace the drain body with one that has a proper internal trap.

  • Pour a cup of water into every floor drain once a week as a habit.
  • Lift the grate and look for hair or scale blocking the outlet; pull it out with pliers.
  • If the drain body itself is a flat plate with no chamber, plan on swapping it for a trapped model — it's a small job but needs the right fitting.

Blocked Roof Ventilation

Your septic system relies on a vent pipe that runs up through the roof. It lets air in so wastewater flows freely by gravity. When that vent gets blocked — bird nests, palm fronds after a storm, accumulated leaves during the rainy season — negative pressure builds in the pipes. That suction literally pulls the water out of your traps while you're showering, and the gas finds its way back in through the lowest opening: the floor drain.

You can often spot the problem from outside: climb onto the roof (or ask your gardener) and look at the top of the white PVC vent pipe. If there's a nest, a tangle of dried grass, or a cap stuck shut, clear it carefully. Make sure the pipe end points upward and has a small rain cap so birds can't re-nest inside.

A quick indoor test: flush the toilet and listen near the sink trap. If you hear a strong 'glug-glug' sucking sound, the vent is restricted somewhere between the tank and the roof.

  • Inspect the roof vent before the first heavy rains each year; wet leaves are heavier and pack tighter.
  • Keep the vent pipe at least 30 cm above the roofline to avoid wind-driven rain entering it.
  • If the vent runs through an attic space, check for kinks or crushed sections where the pipe passes through joists.

A Full Septic Tank

Sanur villas are not connected to a municipal sewer; every property has its own septic tank and soakaway field. When the tank fills past capacity, liquid and gas back up through the drain lines. The smell doesn't stay in one room — you'll notice it in the kitchen, the laundry area, even the garage. Drains start gurgling, toilets flush slowly, and the whole house takes on that thick, organic odour.

This is not something you fix with a plunger or a bottle of enzyme cleaner. The tank needs to be pumped out by a licensed service, and the soakaway may need jetting if it's clogged with fat or solids.

How often pumping is needed depends entirely on how many people use the villa and whether there's a greywater system feeding into the same tank. There is no fixed calendar rule; watch for the symptoms listed above rather than guessing.

  • If two or more drains in the house are slow at the same time, suspect the tank before blaming individual pipes.
  • Avoid pouring cooking oil, coffee grounds, or excessive toilet paper down the drains — they accelerate sludge build-up.
  • Keep a note of when the last pump-out happened so you can track the interval over the years.

How to Keep the Smell Gone for Good

Most recurring bathroom smells in Sanur come back because the underlying trap or vent was only treated, not repaired. A weekly water pour helps, but it doesn't fix a broken seal or a cracked pipe joint.

Here's a simple monthly routine that catches problems early: run every fixture for 30 seconds, lift each floor-drain grate and check for standing water, glance at the roof vent from outside, and listen for gurgling while flushing. If anything looks off, address it that week instead of waiting for the smell to return after a holiday.

And if the villa has been empty for a month or more (a common situation with rental properties), do a full flush-through on day one back: open all taps, run both toilets, pour water into every drain. It takes ten minutes and saves you from waking up to a stinky bathroom.

  • Monthly: visual check of traps, floor drains, and roof vent.
  • After any extended vacancy: full water-flow test through every fixture before settling in.
  • Before rainy season: clear leaves and debris from the vent cap and check the soakaway area for pooling.

Questions

Why does my bathroom only start smelling after heavy rain?

Rain washes leaves, mud, and small branches down into the roof vent pipe. Once the vent is partially blocked, negative pressure pulls water out of your traps during showers, and sewer gas escapes through the floor drain. Clearing the vent usually stops the problem within a day.

Can I just pour bleach or enzyme cleaner down the drain to kill the smell?

It might mask the odour for a few hours, but it won't fix a dry trap, a broken seal, or a full septic tank. And mixing different cleaning chemicals (bleach with acids, for example) can produce toxic fumes — if you ever get a strong chemical or gas-like smell while cleaning, stop immediately, ventilate the room, and call a professional rather than adding more product.

How often should a Sanur villa's septic tank be pumped?

There's no universal schedule. A two-bedroom villa used by one couple may go longer between pump-outs than a four-bedroom rental hosting six guests daily. The reliable indicator is performance: slow drains in multiple rooms, gurgling toilets, or a persistent organic smell that doesn't clear after checking traps and vents all point to a tank that needs attention.
